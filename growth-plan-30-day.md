# The Hormozi read on Refer Labs, and a 30-day plan

Written against the real numbers: ~150 organic clicks/month, average position 39, 50%+ of revenue from one Moshy link, Instagram outperforming search, `/moshy` converting at 13.85% CTR from position 8.

---

## Part 1: What he would say in the first ten minutes

**"Your constraint isn't rankings. It's volume of shots on goal."**

The site converts. `/moshy` sits at position 8 and takes 13.85% of the clicks it sees, which is well above the curve for that position. The Apollo enquiry form works end to end. The quiz routes people correctly. None of that is broken.

You have roughly 150 organic clicks a month. That is the whole funnel. No amount of conversion work fixes a top-of-funnel that small, and you have spent months optimising the part that already works.

**"You picked the slowest channel available and made it your main one."**

SEO on a young domain in health and money is the longest-payback channel that exists. It compounds, so keep it running, but it cannot be the plan. Your own data already says so: Instagram delivered 38 sessions against organic's 20, from a fraction of the effort.

**"You don't own your offer, and that's the real problem."**

You sell someone else's product for a one-off commission. You cannot change the price, the guarantee, the terms, or the funnel. When Moshy changes something, your business changes. Half your revenue depends on a single link you don't control.

**"You have one asset you actually own, and it's broken."**

The email list. It is the only thing here that isn't rented from Google, Instagram or a partner. And right now newsletter storage is silently failing, so subscribers reach your inbox and vanish. In Hormozi terms this is the single most expensive bug on the site, and it isn't close.

---

## Part 2: The three offers you actually have

**Offer 1 (consumer, borrowed):** "Compare providers and get a discount."
Weak by his standard: no pricing power, no ownership, commodity discount. Keep it, it pays the bills, but it will never be the growth engine.

**Offer 2 (list, owned):** the guide and quiz.
This is the one to build. You own the audience, can market repeatedly, and it survives an algorithm change.

**Offer 3 (B2B, owned and priced):** Get Discovered / Generate Leads / Build Distribution.
This is where he would spend most of his attention, and where you are currently spending least. A single lead-gen client is worth more than hundreds of affiliate clicks, it recurs, and you set the price.

### The Grand Slam version of Offer 3
Run the value equation on the lead-gen offer:

| Lever | Move |
|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Not "traffic". "Qualified Australian enquiries from people actively comparing your category, in your inbox." |
| Perceived likelihood | Show the Apollo funnel working. Real GSC positions. Real page-one comparisons. Proof beats adjectives. |
| Time delay | "First leads inside 30 days", not a six-month content build. |
| Effort and sacrifice | "We build the page, the form and the tracking. You take the calls." |
| Risk reversal | **Pay per qualified lead. No lead, no invoice.** |

That last line is the whole offer. It removes the objection that kills every small publisher's B2B pitch, and you can afford it because the infrastructure already exists.

## Part 3: The 30-day plan

Rule for the month: **more and better before new.** No new verticals, no new page types, no new tools. You already have more assets than you are distributing.

### Days 1–3: Fix the leaks, then stop touching the site
1. **Fix newsletter storage.** Everything else is downstream. (Check the Supabase project Vercel actually points at, and that `SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY` is set in production.)
2. **Send the five partner backlink emails.** Ten minutes. Highest-leverage minutes available.
3. **Write the one-page lead-gen offer** using the table above. One page, pay-per-lead, no retainer.
4. **Set the scoreboard.** Three numbers only: content pieces published, outreach conversations started, emails captured. Not rankings. Rankings are a lagging indicator you cannot control daily.

Then close the code editor for the month.

### Days 4–30: Volume on the Core Four

**Warm content — daily, non-negotiable.**
One post a day minimum. Not seven posts in a week and then nothing. He would argue for three to five a day; one a day is the floor at which this works at all.

Do not create, **document**. You have research nobody else in Australia has done: real cover figures, real price checks, real comparisons. Turn each into a post.
- Every comparison page = 3 posts (the verdict, the surprising detail, the mistake people make)
- Every FAQ answer = 1 post
- Every price check = 1 post

That is 100+ pieces sitting in the site already. Repurpose the same piece to Instagram, TikTok, Reddit and LinkedIn. Same content, four channels, one effort.

**Warm outreach — 10 conversations a day.**
Not broadcasts. Actual messages to actual people: past contacts, anyone who has asked you about weight loss or pet insurance, local pet and gym communities. Lead with the guide, never the affiliate link.

**Cold outreach — 10 a day, B2B.**
This is the lead-gen offer. Target Australian brands in your existing categories: pet insurers, battery installers, telehealth. The pitch is one line: *"We send Australians who are actively comparing in your category. Pay per qualified lead, no retainer. Want a test month?"*

**Cold content — one paid test, capped.**
$500 total, one platform, one audience, one creative theme, pointed at the quiz or the guide (not the homepage, not an affiliate link). You are buying data, not sales. If cost-per-email lands under about $2, scale it. If not, kill it and put the effort back into organic.

**Lead getters — the compounding one.**
Ask every partner (Moshy, Mosh, Juniper, Knose, PetsOnMe, Apollo) for two things: a backlink, and whether they will feature the comparison to their own audience. Their list is bigger than yours. This is the closest thing to free distribution you have.

### Weekly rhythm
| | Focus |
|---|---|
| Week 1 | Fix the leaks, ship the offer, start daily posting |
| Week 2 | Hold the posting cadence, start cold B2B outreach, launch the $500 test |
| Week 3 | Double down on the best-performing post format. Kill the worst. |
| Week 4 | Count. Decide what continues into month two based on the numbers, not the feeling. |

## Part 4: What to measure

Weekly, on one line:
- Content pieces published (target: 30+ for the month)
- Conversations started (target: 400+)
- Emails captured (target: 100+)
- B2B calls booked (target: 4+)
- Affiliate revenue (the lagging one)

Not: rankings, impressions, or anything you cannot move today.

---

## Part 5: Where this breaks from Hormozi, honestly

His playbook assumes you own the offer, set the price, and keep the customer. Affiliate arbitrage breaks all three. Applied literally, his advice is "stop being an affiliate and start being the thing", which is a different business.

The honest synthesis: **run affiliate as the cash engine, build the list and the B2B offer as the actual business.** The site is done. The next dollar comes from distribution and from owning something, not from another page.

The uncomfortable summary: you have spent months on a channel that pays back in years, while the channel that already works for you gets used occasionally. Volume, on the thing that already works, is the whole plan.

# Part 6: If he owned it, how he'd get to $20k/month

Assumes $20k/month. Your own per-partner value estimates (from the click tracker) are used below: Moshy $80, Mosh $70, Juniper $50, AiSDR $120, GoHighLevel $100. Those are planning estimates, not confirmed payouts, so verify before betting on them.

## The maths, three ways

**Path A: affiliate only.**
$20,000 ÷ $80 average commission = **250 conversions a month**. At a strong 3% visitor-to-conversion rate that needs roughly 8,300 visitors a month. You currently get about 150 organic clicks. That is a **55x increase in traffic** to hit the number.

He would not take this path. Not because it's impossible, but because the input required is enormous and the output is a one-off payment on a customer you never see again.

**Path B: lead generation.**
A battery installer clears thousands of margin on a $10k-15k system, so a qualified, consented enquiry is worth $150-400 to them. Call it $200.
$20,000 ÷ $200 = **100 leads a month**, about 3-4 a day.
Business lending is better again: a settled commercial loan pays a commission in the thousands, so a handful of settlements moves the number on its own.

**Path C: B2B services.**
5 clients × $4,000/month, or 10 × $2,000. **$20,000.**

## The reframe that decides it

> **You need five clients, not two hundred and fifty customers.**

That is the whole insight. 250 strangers each completing a purchase is a traffic problem you cannot solve this year. Five businesses saying yes is a conversation problem you can solve in ninety days at ten outreach messages a day.

He would go C first for the cash, B second because the infrastructure already exists, and let A compound quietly in the background.

## What he'd change in week one

**1. Price on value, not on effort.**
The instinct is to charge what feels comfortable. He would charge what the outcome is worth to the buyer. A battery installer converting one lead in five, on a system with several thousand in margin, can pay $250-400 a lead and still be delighted. Price there, not at $50 because it feels safer.

**2. Kill the retainer objection with risk reversal.**
"Pay per qualified lead. No lead, no invoice." You can afford it because the Apollo enquiry funnel already works end to end. Almost nobody else pitching a small publisher can say it.

**3. Sell one vertical, not five.**
Home batteries. The lead value is high, the funnel is built, the calculator feeds it, and Apollo already proves the model. One category, one pitch, repeated until it is boring.

**4. Sell to the businesses you already rank for.**
You have page-one comparison pages in health and pet insurance. Every provider in those categories is a prospect who can *see* the traffic you would send them. That is a warmer pitch than any cold list.

## The sequence

**Days 1-90: get to $5k/month, mostly from B2B.**
Ten cold messages a day to Australian battery installers, pet insurers and telehealth brands. One line: *"We send Australians actively comparing in your category. Pay per qualified lead, no retainer. Want a test month?"* At a 2-3% conversion, 900 messages produces roughly 20 conversations and 2-4 clients. That is $5-10k/month.

**Months 4-6: get to $10-12k.**
Keep the two or three clients alive by actually delivering leads, which means volume on content and the $500 paid test scaled if the cost per email works. Add lead-gen in the second vertical only once the first is stable.

**Months 7-12: $20k+.**
Five to eight clients, affiliate compounding underneath as the SEO work from this year finally ranks, and an email list large enough to launch an offer into rather than starting from zero each time.

Twelve months is the realistic timeline from where you are. Six is possible if the outreach volume is genuinely daily and you stop building.

## What he'd kill immediately

- **Business software.** Zero clicks, positions 44-70, and it consumes attention. It is already de-navved; he would stop thinking about it entirely.
- **Any new vertical before the first lead-gen client exists.** Cold plunge, sauna, supplements: all of it waits.
- **More site building.** The site converts. Another page does not move this number.
- **Splitting effort across five categories** to feel diversified while none of them gets enough push to work.

## The risks he would name out loud

**Concentration.** More than half your revenue depends on one partner's link. Lead-gen and the email list are the hedges, which is another reason to build them.

**You are the constraint.** Every path above runs through one person doing outreach daily. The first hire, or the first VA, is what breaks the ceiling, and that is affordable at roughly $5k/month.

**Delivery risk.** Selling leads you cannot deliver is worse than not selling. Take the first client small, over-deliver, then use that result as the proof for the next five.
