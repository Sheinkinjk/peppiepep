# Strength and body composition for women 40+: deep dive

Prepared 3 October 2026. Read-only research. It follows Model 1 in `reports/longevity-business-models-2026-10-03.md`. All URLs were read on 3 October 2026 unless a different date is given. Any figure not read from a primary source is labelled **UNVERIFIED**, **SNIPPET** (seen only in a search-result summary, page not opened) or **ASSUMPTION**.

---

## 0. The answer in one paragraph

**Yes, with conditions, and the conditions shape the product.** Seven of the nine funds publish wording that pays extras benefits on **individual, real-time video consultations** with an exercise physiologist and a dietitian, at the same rate as face-to-face. They are Bupa, Medibank, nib, HBF, Australian Unity, GMHBA and ahm. Teachers Health lists both services as available by telehealth. **HCF is the gap.** Its only published telehealth document for these services ran "until 31 December 2022", and it states it does not cover "online exercise programs (including those delivered through mobile apps)".

Across every fund that publishes a rule:
- **Group or class sessions by telehealth are not paid** (nib and HBF say so explicitly).
- **The app is not claimable.** Only the clinician consult is.
- Each consult must be a **genuine individual session of standard length**. HBF requires at least 45 minutes for an initial EP consult and 30 minutes for a subsequent one.
- The consult must be invoiced under the AEP's or APD's **Medicare provider number**, which is location-specific.

On a mid-tier policy the rebate runs from about **A$26 to A$66 per AEP consult**, inside annual limits of roughly **A$250 to A$600**. Those limits are often shared with physio. That makes the rebate a **conversion aid worth A$30 to A$50 a month to a member**. It does not underwrite the business. Nothing found says a fund will pay for a *prevention* program for healthy women. The fund-paid programs found all target conditions that drive hospital claims (joint replacement, cardiac, obesity with chronic disease), and all require hospital cover.

### 1.2 What the law says

- **PHI Act 2007 s121-10, "general treatment":** treatment "intended to manage or prevent a disease, injury or condition" that is not hospital treatment, plus anything the Rules add. This is SNIPPET wording from AustLII, which blocked the direct fetch. The current compilation is dated 19 Sep 2026 ([legislation.gov.au](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2007A00031/latest/text)). The Act does not mention the mode of delivery. Telehealth is a **fund rule**, not a statutory one. Each fund decides by modality, which is why Bupa's terms say approval "will be published on Bupa's website".
- **"Prevent… a condition"** is the statutory hook a prevention-framed program needs. ahm reads the Act more narrowly for classes and programs: "Under the Private Health Insurance Act, we can only pay benefits for exercise classes… to assist with the management and treatment of already identified health conditions… not for general fitness or wellbeing." Individual EP consults are not caught by that sentence, but programs and classes are.
- **The Health Insurance Business Rules** were not read in full; the 2018 Rules compilation was not opened. **UNVERIFIED** whether any Rule speaks to telehealth. No evidence was found that one does.
- **Did the 2020 arrangements become permanent?** The 2020 change was an **ACCC authorisation (AA1000487)** letting PHA, the Members Health Fund Alliance and named insurers coordinate on broadening cover to "treatment received by telephone or videoconferencing". It was granted interim on 8 Apr 2020 and finalised 17 Sep 2020. PHA withdrew its revocation and substitution application on 8 Apr 2021 (SNIPPET, from the [ACCC register](https://www.accc.gov.au/public-registers/authorisations-and-notifications-registers/authorisations-register/private-healthcare-australia-limited-0)). After that, each fund decided alone:
  - Australian Unity made it permanent from 1 Apr 2021.
  - GMHBA and Teachers say "permanent".
  - Bupa's list has no end date.
  - HBF ended podiatry only (31 Dec 2021).
  - HCF's published form ended 31 Dec 2022.
  - So: **permanent at most funds, by fund decision, not by law.**
- **Private Health Insurance Ombudsman:** no telehealth-specific publication found. The Ombudsman's reports say general-treatment complaints are mostly disputes over amounts and fund benefit rules ([complaints page](https://www.ombudsman.gov.au/complaints/private-health-insurance-complaints), SNIPPET). Practical reading: a rejected telehealth claim goes to the fund first, then the Ombudsman, so the risk sits on whether the provider followed the fund's telehealth rules.

### 1.3 Provider requirements, assembled

1. **AEP:** ESSA accreditation, then a **Services Australia (Medicare) provider number, which is location-specific**, then automatic recognition at Bupa, GMHBA and Australian Unity ("Medicare will then send us your details"). **APD:** Dietitians Australia credential plus a Medicare provider number.
2. **Private practice:** Bupa requires private practice and defines it as operating "on an independent and self-supporting basis… sole trader, partnership, corporate or group practice". A company employing or contracting AEPs qualifies.
3. **Location.** Bupa terms limit services to "the location to which your Provider Number applies, unless otherwise approved". Receipts must show the "address where the Provider performed the service (including… Telehealth)". **Open operational question:** can a contracted AEP consulting from home bill under a provider number for the company's registered premises, or do they need one for their home address? Ask Services Australia and Bupa. **UNVERIFIED.**
4. **Item numbers:** use the telehealth item "specified by your Professional Body, HICAPS or HealthPoint, or Bupa" (Bupa terms, cl. 6(a)(v)). HCF used 710/712 (EP) and 301/302 (dietetics). ahm uses "exercise physiology by teleconsultation".
5. **HICAPS:** Bupa announced on-the-spot HICAPS claiming for telehealth EP and dietetics ([Bupa media](https://media.bupa.com.au/bupa-leads-the-way-to-instant-telehealth-rebates/), SNIPPET). Other funds mostly have members claim afterwards in the fund's app ("We can't swipe your card over video", [Beachside EP](https://beachsideep.com.au/funding/telehealth/)). Point-of-sale gap is friction: the member pays full price first.
6. **Clinical gates:**
   - Bupa requires a "diagnosed clinical condition" for telehealth, and a GP referral or a suitability assessment for new patients.
   - ahm says new telehealth patients "should consult with your GP".
   - So intake should record a qualifying reason (for example osteopenia, low muscle mass, joint pain, menopausal symptoms affecting function), not "general fitness".

### 1.4 The approved-program and fund-funded route (B2B)

- **HCF Health Management Programs:** up to A$50 (Mid) or A$200 (Top) a year. Approved categories are Childbirth Education, Lactation, Learn to Swim, Quit Smoking, Stress Management and Weight Management. The form asks whether the program is delivered face to face, and HCF excludes app-delivered exercise programs. **A strength program does not fit a listed category, and the money is small.**
- **Bupa Health Management:** weight programs "should be based on face-to-face consultations… with program providers registered with Bupa". Claims go by post or in a store only.
- **Teachers Health Healthy Lifestyle:** already pays for online fitness programs with an ABN once a practitioner signs the form. **This is the most open door found for an app-led product.**
- **Fund-funded programs.** nib fully funds the **Kieser Hip and Knee OA Program** for 15 weeks: 26 supervised strength sessions plus the Kieser Konnect app. Eligibility needs a diagnosis "likely to require joint replacement in the next three years" and 12 months of hospital cover. nib also funds **Healthy Weight for Life** (chronic disease plus BMI 28+) ([nib page](https://www.nib.com.au/health-management-programs/muscle-joint-bone-health-programs)). HCF runs **Osteoarthritis Healthy Weight for Life** with Prima Health Solutions. Bupa runs in-house phone coaching by nurses and dietitians, at no cost, for members with hospital cover ([Bupa Telehealth](https://www.bupa.com.au/telehealth)).
- **The pattern:** funds buy programs that **reduce hospital claims** (joint replacement, cardiac readmission, bariatric). A perimenopause strength program would need to argue **fracture and falls avoidance** over a long horizon. That is a weaker and slower case. **No fund menopause program was found.**
- **Employer channel:** Medibank signed a three-year partnership with League, focused first on "Medibank's new corporate digital health and wellbeing solutions" ([Medibank newsroom](https://www.medibank.com.au/livebetter/newsroom/post/medibank-and-league-partner-to-transform-the-digital-health-experience), SNIPPET for detail). Medibank also launched Amplar Health Online Doctor in June 2026 (SNIPPET). Medibank's Live Better rewards were not verified.
- **Recognition timeline:** for an individual AEP, recognition is automatic once the Medicare provider number issues. Services Australia's processing time was not read (**UNVERIFIED**). For an approved program or fund contract, no published timeline was found. Assume months, and expect evidence (outcome data) to be required.

## 2. Medicare

From [MBS 10953](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=10953&qt=item) (EP), [10954](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=10954&qt=item) (dietetics) and [93000](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=93000&qt=item) (video). Schedule fees updated 1 Jul 2026.

- **Criteria:**
  - The patient "has a chronic condition and complex care needs" managed by a GP under a **GP chronic condition management plan** prepared or reviewed in the last 18 months. Old GPMP/TCA plans made before 1 Jul 2025 count until 30 Jun 2027.
  - The service is recommended in the plan.
  - The session runs at least 20 minutes.
- **Visits:** a maximum of **5 allied health services per calendar year across all disciplines combined** (EP and dietetics share the five).
- **Fee:** A$74.55; **benefit A$63.40** (85%).
- **Telehealth:** 93000 covers video. It must be individual, referred, and followed by a written report to the referring GP.
- **Fit:** useful for women with osteoporosis or osteopenia, osteoarthritis, type 2 diabetes and similar conditions. It does **not** cover wellness. Funds do not pay extras on a service Medicare has paid, and Bupa's guidelines exclude a patient "eligible to receive… benefits for the service from another source (such as Medicare)". Medicare is a 5-session on-ramp, not a revenue line.

## 4. Regulation

- **Ahpra:** AEPs and APDs are not among the 16 Ahpra-regulated professions ([Ahpra](https://www.ahpra.gov.au/About-Ahpra/Who-We-Are/Ahpra-and-the-National-Boards.aspx), carried from the prior report). National Law s133 (advertising, including the testimonial ban) does not apply to them. It **does** apply the moment a physio, GP or psychologist is used or advertised.
- **ESSA Code of Professional Conduct and Ethical Practice:** breach of ESSA standards "may constitute a breach of the ESSA Code" and lead to disciplinary action, including loss of accreditation, which kills fund recognition. The code text was not read. **UNVERIFIED** what it says about advertising.
- **National Code of Conduct for health care workers:** it applies to unregistered health workers including AEPs and APDs, and is enforced by state health complaints bodies (prohibition orders). Confirmed in force:
  - Queensland (from 1 Oct 2015, [Qld Health](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/system-governance/policies-standards/national-code-of-conduct), SNIPPET).
  - Tasmania ("started on 24 June 2026", SNIPPET).
  - SA (aligned code, SNIPPET).
  - WA described as "joining" (HaDSCO 2025 sheet, SNIPPET).
  - NSW, Victoria, ACT and NT: not confirmed in this pass.
  - The code includes not making false or misleading claims about health benefits. Since the service is national, design to the strictest version.
- **ACL:** applies in full. s18 (misleading conduct), s29 (false representations, including s29(1)(e) testimonials) and s33-34. Every claim about bone density, menopause symptoms or muscle needs substantiation. Testimonials must be real and consented.
- **Privacy:** "The small business exemption does not apply" to an entity that "provides a health service… and holds any health information". The OAIC lists "allied health professionals… gyms and weight loss clinics" as health service providers ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/health-information/what-is-a-health-service-provider), SNIPPET). So the APPs apply from day one: consent for sensitive information, cross-border disclosure (APP 8) for any offshore app or video vendor, and the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme. Bupa's terms also require a secure video platform compliant with the Privacy Act.
- **TGA, the app.** Item 14C excludes software "intended by its manufacturer to be used by a consumer to improve general health or wellness by coaching, or encouraging behavioural change, in relation to… weight, exercise… or dietary intake" and **not** intended "in clinical practice or to provide information… that would generally be accepted to require the interpretation of a health professional" ([TGA 14C guidance](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-behavioural-change-or-coaching-software-exclusion)). **Where the line sits:**
  - A consumer program app (workouts, habit nudges, protein targets) is excluded.
  - If AEPs use the app to make treatment decisions for a condition, the "clinical practice" limb can take it outside 14C.
  - Claims about a **serious** condition (osteoporosis, diabetes) also break the exclusion.
  - Design rule: the app delivers the plan the AEP wrote; it does not diagnose, score fracture risk or make treatment decisions. App copy speaks of strength and habits, never of treating osteoporosis.
  - Have this confirmed in the legal review.
- **Clinical safety (perimenopause):**
  - Use ESSA's **Adult Pre-exercise Screening System (APSS, v2 2019)**. Stage 1 asks about heart condition or stroke, unexplained chest pain, faintness or dizziness during exercise, an asthma attack needing medical care in 12 months, poorly controlled diabetes, and other conditions ([APSS user guide](https://ausactive.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Adult-Pre-Exercise-Screening-System-Guide.pdf), SNIPPET).
  - Population-specific red flags to add (clinical judgement, **ASSUMPTION** pending AEP review): known osteoporosis or prior fragility fracture (load and spinal-flexion progressions), pelvic floor dysfunction and prolapse, abnormal or post-menopausal bleeding (refer to GP), palpitations, uncontrolled hypertension, and current cancer treatment.
  - Telehealth adds a home-environment check (Bupa requires the patient's space to be "free of obstruction, with all risks and hazards removed").

The government response came in Feb 2025 ([response PDF](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-02/government-response-to-inquiry-issues-related-to-menopause-and-perimenopause.pdf), SNIPPET for contents).
- **Funding:** A$64.5M over three years from 2025-26 for specialist women's health care focused on perimenopause and menopause ([Minister](https://www.health.gov.au/ministers/the-hon-mark-butler-mp/media/delivering-the-first-national-menopause-campaign-for-australian-women?language=en), SNIPPET). New Medicare-funded menopause health assessments from 1 July 2025 (SNIPPET). These create GP touchpoints that can refer to an AEP.
- **Search trends:** carried from the prior report ("creatine for women" +86% YoY). AU volume for "menopause exercise" and related terms was **not pulled**.

**Members needed for A$1M revenue a year:** A$1,788 a year per member means **about 560 average active members**. At 10% monthly churn that needs about 56 new members a month to stand still, and about 670 gross adds in a year. At A$250 CAC, that is about **A$170k of acquisition a year** against gross profit of about A$250k to A$370k (contractor) or about A$570k (employed). The model works only with employed or salaried AEPs at good utilisation, group or async leverage outside the claimable consult, or a B2B buyer. That is the same conclusion as the prior report, now with sourced contractor rates showing the contractor-only version is thin.

**Design consequences of the fund rules:**
1. The claimable unit is the **individual consult**. Keep it real: right length, real time, item-coded, notes kept. Never price the consult up to carry the app; fund audits would treat that as an invoicing breach.
2. Group classes and the app sit outside the rebate. Use them for margin and retention, not for the claim.
3. Have the intake record a qualifying condition, so the service passes Bupa's "diagnosed clinical condition" gate and ahm's "identified health condition" language.
4. Point-of-sale claiming exists only with Bupa via HICAPS. Elsewhere the member claims afterwards in the fund's app, so build a "claim your rebate" receipt flow into the product.
