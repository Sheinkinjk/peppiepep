# Licensed verticals assessment: consumer credit, insurance, private health insurance, business lending

Prepared 19 September 2026 for Pepform Pty Ltd trading as Refer Labs (ABN 32 660 008 159).

**This is research, not legal advice.** It reads the statutes, regulations and regulator guidance directly and reasons from them. Several conclusions turn on questions of degree that only a court, a written ASIC position or a lawyer's opinion can settle. Those points are listed in section 10 and must be confirmed before any page is built or restored on the strength of this document.

Fixed inputs, not revisited here: the operator cannot hold an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL) or an Australian Credit Licence (ACL). The business's only proven mechanism is a consumer-side incentive: every recorded transaction came from a page carrying a discount code or promotional offer.

---

## 1. The answer

**No.** There is no configuration in which consumer mortgages, general or life insurance, or private health insurance clears A$200,000 net a year for this publisher at anything near its current scale, and the one vertical where the law and the proven mechanism line up (private health insurance) has no published referral rate to model against.

The reasons, in the order they bite:

1. **Mortgages and consumer credit are closed to the proven mechanism.** Without an ACL the only lawful role is a bare referral under reg 25(2A) of the National Consumer Credit Protection Regulations 2010, which covers an activity that "consists only of" telling the consumer a licensee can help, how to contact it, and what the referrer is paid. Attaching a discount or cashback to a particular lender's product is a suggestion that the consumer apply for "a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider" (NCCP Act s8(a)), which is credit assistance and needs a licence. The one sourced referral rate for a website-compatible program is A$200 to A$350 per settled home loan.
2. **General and life insurance allow an inducement, but pay too little per policy.** An unlicensed referrer can rely on reg 7.6.01(1)(e) of the Corporations Regulations 2001, and nothing in the Corporations Act or ASIC Act prohibits passing on an insurer-funded promotion, provided it is honoured and the advertising, design and distribution rules are met. The sourced rates are A$50 per pet policy (PetsOnMe) and about A$25 per travel policy (Insure&Go). At A$50 the site would need 4,000 policies a year, which is 128% of every organic click the whole site currently receives.
3. **Private health insurance is the least restricted vertical and the least verifiable.** It is not a financial product (Corporations Act s765A(1)(c)), so no AFSL is needed to compare or refer it. Insurer-funded joining promotions are allowed but capped by rule 6 of the Private Health Insurance (Complying Product) Rules 2015 (disregarded only up to 12% of the first year's full premium). No Australian health fund's affiliate rate could be read from a public page, so the rate is UNVERIFIED. At a hypothetical A$200 per policy the site would need 1,000 policies a year; at A$100, 2,000.
4. **The site does not have the traffic.** Search Console, 21 August to 17 September 2026, read on 19 September 2026: **240 clicks and 26,366 impressions sitewide** (the brief's ~29k and ~250 figures are close to this pull; the difference is aggregation, not a change). Queries touching insurance, loans, mortgages or health funds drew 2,542 impressions and **0 clicks**, at average positions mostly between 25 and 75. Annualised, the whole site receives about 3,129 organic clicks a year.

The arithmetic is in section 8. The short form: every sourced rate needs between 571 and 7,962 conversions a year to reach A$200,000, against 3,129 clicks a year for the entire site across every vertical.

## 4. Consumer credit and mortgages

### 4.1 What triggers the licence

NCCP Act s29(1): "A person must not engage in a credit activity if the person does not hold a licence authorising the person to engage in the credit activity." Civil penalty 5,000 penalty units; s29(2) makes it an offence carrying 2 years imprisonment.

A credit activity includes providing a "credit service" (s6(1), table item 2). A credit service is providing credit assistance or acting as an intermediary (s7).

**Credit assistance, s8**, verbatim in the parts that matter:

> A person provides credit assistance to a consumer if, by dealing directly with the consumer or the consumer's agent in the course of, as part of, or incidentally to, a business carried on in this jurisdiction by the person or another person, the person:
> (a) suggests that the consumer apply for a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider; or
> ...
> (c) suggests that the consumer remain in a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider; or
> (d) assists the consumer to apply for a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider; ...

**Acting as an intermediary, s9(a)**: acting "as an intermediary (whether directly or indirectly) between a credit provider and a consumer wholly or partly for the purposes of securing a provision of credit for the consumer under a credit contract for the consumer with the credit provider". Unlike s8, this does not need a particular product: ASIC says "You can be acting as an intermediary before the consumer has made a decision about what particular credit contract or consumer lease they should apply for" (RG 203.74).

### 4.2 ASIC's reading of "suggests"

RG 203.61: "The term 'suggest' is not defined in the National Credit Act. We consider that it involves proposing to a consumer, or introducing into their mind, the idea that they should: (a) apply for a particular credit contract or consumer lease with a particular credit provider or lessor ..."

RG 203.62: "'suggesting' can extend to offering a consumer advice or making a recommendation".

RG 203.63: whether a suggestion is credit assistance "may be affected by the circumstances in which your suggestions are made, including statements made and other conduct engaged in by you in the promotion of your services."

RG 203.64, Example 5: a general suggestion to borrow is not credit assistance, but "a suggestion that a consumer approach a particular finance company or bank about a specific product would be credit assistance, even if the person making the suggestion does not arrange or help with setting up the credit contract." Where the provider offers "a very limited range of credit products", naming the provider alone may still be credit assistance.

RG 203 Example 10 (comparison websites, under RG 203.75): a site comparing rates and features and linking to credit providers' application forms "may be acting as an intermediary", and "is more likely to be considered to be giving the information and referrals for this purpose if they receive a benefit based on the consumer applying". The note adds that where the comparison "identifies the existing product or other particular products as being the best product for the consumer", the operator could also be providing credit assistance, while generic information that is the same for everyone, even if sortable, is "less likely" to be.

RG 203 Example 12 (under RG 203.79): a disclaimer that the site "does not provide credit assistance", sitting beside statements that amount to suggestions, "is likely to be misleading". A disclaimer does not change what the conduct is.

### 4.3 The referrer exemptions, verbatim

**Reg 25(2A)**, the website-link ("downstream") exemption. This is the only one that fits a publisher:

> (2A) A credit activity is exempted if:
> (a) the activity consists only of:
> (i) a person (person 1) informing another person (person 2) that a licensee or registered person, or a representative of the licensee or registered person, is able to provide a particular credit activity or a class of credit activities; and
> (ii) person 1 giving person 2 information about how person 2 may contact the licensee, registered person or representative; and
> (iii) person 1 making arrangements enabling person 2 to contact the licensee, registered person or representative by means of a link that can be accessed from a website provided by or for person 1 or an associate of person 1; and
> (b) at the time the activity is engaged in, person 1 discloses to person 2:
> (i) any benefits, including indirect remuneration, that person 1, or an associate of person 1, may receive in respect of the activity; and
> (ii) any benefits, including indirect remuneration, that person 1, or an associate of person 1, may receive that are attributable to the activity; and
> (c) the disclosure mentioned in paragraph (b) is provided in the same form as the information mentioned in subparagraph (a)(ii).

Reg 25(2) is the same without the website link (paragraph (a)(iii)).

**Reg 25(5)**, the lead-passing ("upstream") exemption, requires all of: a written agreement with the licensee specifying the conduct (25(5)(a), (b)); the activity consisting only of informing the consumer, passing "the consumer's name and contact details within 5 business days", and "a short description of the purpose" (25(5)(c)); benefit disclosure at the time (25(5)(e)); no fee charged to the consumer (25(5)(f)); the consumer's consent (25(5)(g)); and:

> (h) the referrer engages in the activity as a matter incidental to the carrying on of a business that is not principally making contact with persons for the purpose of giving their names or other details to another person; and
> (i) the referrer does not conduct a business as part of which the referrer contacts persons face‑to‑face from non‑standard business premises.

Reg 9AB then binds the licensee receiving upstream referrals: it must keep a register of referrers (9AB(2), (3)), contact the consumer within 10 business days (9AB(5)), and open the conversation by naming the referrer and stating that "[name of referrer] may receive the following financial benefits" (9AB(6)(b)).

ASIC's summary, RG 203.117: "You cannot rely on these exemptions if you engage in broader activities that are credit activities. For example, if, in addition to informing the consumer that the other person can provide particular credit activities and passing on contact details, you suggest the consumer contact that person to apply for a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider, you will not be able to rely on these exemptions."

### 4.4 What may be passed, disclosed and received

| Question | Answer, with provision |
|---|---|
| What information may pass? | Downstream (25(2), 25(2A)): only the fact that a licensee can provide a credit activity or class of activities, how to contact it, and a website link. Nothing flows from the consumer to the lender through the referrer. Upstream (25(5)): the consumer's name, contact details and "a short description of the purpose", within 5 business days, with consent, under a written agreement. |
| What disclosure? | Any benefit, including indirect remuneration, the referrer or an associate "may receive in respect of" or "that are attributable to" the activity, made "at the time the activity is engaged in" and, for downstream, "in the same form" as the contact information (25(2)(c), 25(2A)(c)). RG 203.119: if contact information is given in writing, the commission disclosure must be in writing. |
| May a fee be received? | Yes, from the licensee, provided it is disclosed as above. The referrer must not require the consumer to pay a fee for the referral (25(4)(e), 25(5)(f)). |
| Can a lead-generation business use the upstream route? | No. 25(5)(h) excludes a business "principally making contact with persons for the purpose of giving their names or other details to another person", and RG 203.121(c) repeats it. A publisher whose credit pages exist to generate leads would fail it. The downstream exemption has no equivalent "incidental" condition. |

### 4.5 Where a comparison page crosses the line

| Page element | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| General explanation of how home loans, comparison rates or LVR work, naming no provider | Not a credit activity | RG 203.64 Example 5; RG 203 Example 7 ("information about market interest rates and desirable features of home loans would not be credit assistance if ... [not related] to specific products") |
| A link to a licensee (for example a mortgage broker), with the commission disclosed in the same form | Exempt under reg 25(2A) | Referring to a broker who offers many lenders is not suggesting a particular credit contract with a particular credit provider; RG 203.119 Example 14 treats a real estate agent sending a consumer to "a particular finance broker" through a website link, with the commission disclosed, as "likely to be covered by the downstream referral exemption" |
| A table of named lenders' products and rates with commission-paying "apply" links, no ranking | Likely acting as an intermediary (s9); exemption uncertain | RG 203 Example 10: "more likely" to be intermediary conduct where the benefit depends on the consumer applying. Reg 25(2A) may still cover a bare link, but the table itself is more than "informing that a licensee ... is able to provide a particular credit activity". This is the grey zone |
| "Best home loan", "top pick", "our pick for first home buyers", a recommended product, or a quiz that outputs a product | Credit assistance (s8(a)) | RG 203.61 to 203.62 (introducing the idea that the consumer should apply for a particular contract with a particular provider); RG 203 Example 10 note on identifying a "best product for the consumer" |
| A lender-specific code, cashback or bonus presented with a link | Credit assistance (s8(a)); outside reg 25(2A) | The offer attaches to one lender's product and exists to make the consumer apply for it. That is "suggesting", and the activity no longer "consists only of" informing and linking (RG 203.117) |
| A form that collects the consumer's details and passes them to lenders | Intermediary (s9); upstream exemption only with a written agreement per lender and only if the business is not principally lead generation (reg 25(5)(h)) | RG 203 Example 9; RG 203.120 to 203.121 |
| A disclaimer saying "we do not provide credit assistance" | Changes nothing, and may itself mislead | RG 203.79, Example 12 |

**Why the seventeen consumer lending pages ran into trouble.** A consumer lending comparison page that earns money does so by getting the reader to apply to a named lender through a tracked link. Each of the three things such a page normally carries takes it out of the only exemption available without a licence: a named-product comparison with commission links is intermediary conduct (s9; RG 203 Example 10); any ranking, "best" label or recommendation is a suggestion to apply for a particular contract with a particular provider (s8(a); RG 203.61 to 203.62); and a lender-specific offer is the same suggestion in commercial form. Reg 25(2A) covers an activity that "consists only of" informing and linking, and RG 203.117 says expressly that adding a suggestion loses the exemption. What remains lawful (a general explainer plus a disclosed link to a broker) does not rank for "compare home loans" and carries no reason for the reader to click.

### 4.6 Can a credit referrer offer the consumer an inducement?

**No, for anything tied to a particular lender's product.** There is no NCCP provision that prohibits inducements as such; the conflicted remuneration rules (NCCP Act s158N; Regs Part 3-9, regs 28VA to 28VM) apply to benefits given to licensees and credit representatives, not to consumers or unlicensed referrers. The obstacle is the definition of the activity: an inducement to apply to a named lender is a suggestion under s8(a), and the referrer becomes a provider of credit assistance who needs a licence or a credit representative authorisation. Neither is available.

**Conditional, for a broker's own offer.** Whether a referrer may tell readers that a licensed mortgage broker offers its own cashback, and link to the broker with the referral fee disclosed, is not answered by the text. The broker is not "a particular credit provider", so s8(a) may not be engaged, but the referrer's activity must still "consist only of" informing and linking. This needs a lawyer's view (section 10, item 1).

The ASIC Act applies throughout: a credit facility is a financial product for the ASIC Act (s12BAA(7)(k)), so s12DA (misleading or deceptive conduct) and s12DE (offering a rebate, gift or prize "with the intention of not providing it, or not providing it as offered") apply to any offer the referrer publishes. The National Credit Code s154 separately prohibits false or misleading representations "in attempting to induce another person to enter into a credit contract".

## 5. General and life insurance

### 5.1 Definitions

Corporations Act s766A(1): a person provides a financial service if they "(a) provide financial product advice; or (b) deal in a financial product". s911A(1): a person carrying on a financial services business "must hold an Australian financial services licence covering the provision of the financial services".

s766B(1): "Financial product advice means a recommendation or a statement of opinion, or a report of either of those things, that: (a) is intended to influence a person or persons in making a decision in relation to a particular financial product or class of financial products ...; or (b) could reasonably be regarded as being intended to have such an influence." General advice is financial product advice that is not personal advice (s766B(4)).

s766C(1) lists dealing (applying for, acquiring, issuing, varying, disposing). s766C(2): "Arranging for a person to engage in conduct referred to in subsection (1) is also dealing in a financial product, unless the actions concerned amount to providing financial product advice."

ASIC on arranging, RG 36.42: "Arranging occurs when a person brings into effect the issue, variation, disposal or acquisition of, or application for, a financial product." RG 36.43 lists indicators, including where "you receive benefits depending on the decisions made by the person for whom you are acting". RG 36.46, Table 3: "Receipt of benefits based on sales ... is an indication of the intermediary's motivation to effect a transaction and may suggest a more active involvement". ASIC calls the test "a question of degree" (RG 36.45).

### 5.2 The referral exemption, verbatim

Corporations Regulations reg 7.6.01(1), made for s911A(2)(k), exempts:

> (e) a financial service provided by a person (person 1) in the following circumstances:
> (i) the service consists only of:
> (A) informing a person (person 2) that a financial services licensee, or a representative of the financial services licensee, is able to provide a particular financial service, or a class of financial services; and
> (B) giving person 2 information about how person 2 may contact the financial services licensee or representative;
> (ii) person 1 is not a representative of the financial service licensee, or of a related body corporate of the financial services licensee;
> (iii) person 1 discloses to person 2, when the service is provided:
> (A) any benefits (including commission) that person 1, or an associate of person 1, may receive in respect of the service; and
> (B) any benefits (including commission) that person 1, or an associate of person 1, may receive that are attributable to the service;
> (iv) the disclosure mentioned in subparagraph (iii) is provided in the same form as the information mentioned in subparagraph (i);

ASIC restates it at RG 36.72 to 36.73. Reg 7.1.33H, sometimes cited as a referral exemption, is not one: it applies only to "a financial product issuer" giving general advice about its own products. Reg 7.1.33G (general advice not about a particular product that attracts no related remuneration) cannot apply to a commissioned referral page because of its paragraph (d).

**Where the line is.** The exemption covers the link and the benefit disclosure. It does not cover a recommendation or a statement of opinion about which policy is better, because that is financial product advice under s766B(1) and the service then no longer "consists only of" informing and giving contact details.

### 5.3 General advice by a publisher

s911A(2)(eb) exempts general advice where "(i) the advice is provided in the course of, or by means of, transmissions that the person makes by means of an information service ...; (ii) the transmissions are generally available to the public; (iii) the sole or principal purpose of the transmissions is not the provision of financial product advice". Reg 7.6.01B(6)(c) confirms "a service provided by the Internet" is an information service. Reg 7.6.01B(1) makes the exemption conditional on stating "any remuneration the person ... is to receive for providing the advice" and any pecuniary interest, presented so it "will adequately bring it to the attention of a reasonable person" (7.6.01B(2)), unless the publisher follows an industry code, the Press Council principles or a board-approved internal policy dealing with financial conflicts (7.6.01B(3)).

Whether a page titled as a comparison of two insurers, whose revenue depends on the click, has a "principal purpose" other than financial product advice is arguable either way. The site as a whole plainly is not principally an advice service; an individual insurance comparison page is closer to the line. This is item 2 in section 10.

### 5.4 Advertising rules that bind the referrer

s1018A(1) applies to "a person", not just the issuer. A person must only advertise a financial product available to retail clients, or "publish a statement that is reasonably likely to induce people to acquire the product", if the advertisement or statement "(c) identifies ... the issuer of the product", "(ca) ... describes the target market for the product or specifies where the determination is available", "(d) indicates that a Product Disclosure Statement for the product is available and where it can be obtained" and "(e) indicates that a person should consider the Product Disclosure Statement". Breach is an offence (s1311(1)).

### 5.5 Design and distribution obligations (Part 7.8A, RG 274)

The unlicensed referrer is inside the regime. Part 7.8A's "regulated person" (s994A(1)) includes "a regulated person as defined in section 1011B", and s1011B(e)(ii) includes "any person who is not required to hold an Australian financial services licence because the person is covered by ... an exemption in regulations made for the purposes of paragraph 911A(2)(k)". Reg 7.6.01(1)(e) is such an exemption. RG 274.28 (note) says so directly: "Persons who refer consumers to another person who engages in financial services or credit activities ('referrers') are regulated persons if they fall under one or more of the categories listed above, such as persons exempt from holding an AFS licence or a credit licence: see reg 7.6.01(1)(e) and (ea) ... and reg 25(2), (2A) and (5) of the National Consumer Credit Protection Regulations 2010".

Consequence: s994E(3) requires a regulated person engaging in "retail product distribution conduct" (dealing, including arranging, or advice: s994A(1)) to take "reasonable steps that would have resulted in, or would have been reasonably likely to have resulted in, the retail product distribution conduct being consistent with the determination". RG 274.209, Example 16: a comparison website is "more likely to amount to 'arranging' if the website provider receives a benefit based on sales". RG 274.210 limits what is expected of an unlicensed referrer: reasonable steps "would not extend to requiring the unlicensed referrer to do something they are not permitted to do under the exemption they rely on." s994D prohibits distribution where no TMD has been made.

On promotions, RG 274.152, Example 14: where "the promotional feature is concessional (e.g. a reduced or nil annual fee), it is likely that the existing target market will remain appropriate", but if it "is likely to encourage consumers outside the target market to acquire the product" the issuer must review the TMD. A price discount is the concessional case.

### 5.6 Hawking (s992A as substituted; RG 38)

s992A(1) prohibits offering a financial product, or requesting or inviting the consumer to apply for one, to a retail client "in the course of, or because of, an unsolicited contact". Unsolicited contact is contact "wholly or partly" by "(i) a telephone call; (ii) a face‑to‑face meeting; (iii) any other real‑time interaction in the nature of a discussion or conversation" without qualifying consent (s992A(4)).

A published web page is not real-time contact. RG 38.34 to 38.35: communications "not in real time" are outside the prohibition, and Example 6 treats unsolicited bulk emails as not hawking. RG 38 Example 14 (under RG 38.68) treats a price comparison website that obtains clear consent before insurers phone the user as solicited. RG 38.77 and Example 18 put the risk of defective consent gathered by "a third party promoter or lead generator" on the insurer, which is why insurers scrutinise lead-generation partners. **For a publisher that links, hawking is not the constraint. It becomes one the moment the model involves phone callbacks or live chat.** Private health insurance is not a financial product, so s992A does not apply to it at all.

### 5.7 Conflicted remuneration (Part 7.7A)

s963A(1) defines conflicted remuneration as a benefit "given to a financial services licensee, or a representative of a financial services licensee, who provides financial product advice to persons as retail clients". An unlicensed person relying on reg 7.6.01(1)(e) is neither (7.6.01(1)(e)(ii) requires that it is not a representative), so the ban does not bite on the referral fee itself. For licensees, s963B(1)(a) excludes general insurance commissions from the ban (subject to s963BB informed consent) and s963B(1)(b) permits life risk commissions only within benefit-ratio and clawback limits. RG 36 Table 3 note: a sales-based benefit "could amount to conflicted remuneration if the intermediary is providing financial product advice to a retail client". Whether a life insurer may lawfully pay an unlicensed referrer at all, given its own commission caps, was not verified (section 10, item 8).

### 5.8 Can an insurance referrer offer the consumer an inducement?

**Conditional yes.** Nothing read prohibits a referrer from publishing an insurer-funded promotion (a code, free months, a premium discount) with its link. The conditions, each with its source:

- The offer must be real and honoured: ASIC Act s12DE(1) (offering a rebate, gift or prize "with the intention of not providing it") and s12DE(2A) (failing to provide it within the stated or a reasonable time); s12DA and s12DB on misleading conduct and false representations.
- The page must meet s1018A(1)(c) to (e): name the issuer, say where the PDS is and that it should be considered, and describe or point to the TMD.
- The referrer must take DDO reasonable steps (s994E(3)); a concessional price promotion is the case ASIC treats as unlikely to move the target market (RG 274.152, Example 14).
- The page must stay a referral (reg 7.6.01(1)(e)) or qualify for the publisher exemption (s911A(2)(eb), reg 7.6.01B). A code attached to a "which is better" verdict combines an inducement with financial product advice.
- A referrer-run "refer a friend" reward contingent on a later event is prohibited: ASIC Act s12DH (referral selling).
- No phone or live-chat follow-up without s992A-compliant consent.

Unlike private health insurance, general insurance has no statutory cap on the size of an insurer's joining promotion. Knose's 2 months free is roughly 17% of a year's premium; the equivalent offer from a health fund would be over the PHI cap.

### 5.9 The live pet insurance pages against these rules

These are observations for the lawyer, not findings of breach:

- `InsuranceDisclosure` states that Refer Labs may receive a commission and points to "the provider's Product Disclosure Statement (PDS) and Target Market Determination (TMD)". s1018A(1)(d) requires saying where the PDS "can be obtained", and (ca) where the TMD is available. Whether a general pointer satisfies both on each page needs checking.
- `/knose-vs-petsonme` opens "On the published numbers Knose is the stronger headline product". That is a statement of opinion about a particular financial product (s766B(1)). It cannot rest on reg 7.6.01(1)(e), which covers only informing and contact details, so it needs s911A(2)(eb) and reg 7.6.01B.
- Both codes are insurer-side offers (Knose's free months; PetsOnMe's pet care services discount). On the analysis in 5.8, publishing them is permissible if the conditions above hold.

## 6. Private health insurance

### 6.1 It is outside the Corporations Act and the ASIC Act

Corporations Act s765A(1): "the following are not financial products: ... (c) health insurance provided as part of a health insurance business (as defined in Division 121 of the Private Health Insurance Act 2007)". ASIC Act s12BAA(7)(d)(i) excludes the same insurance from the ASIC Act's consumer protection definition. So there is no AFSL requirement, no reg 7.6.01 condition, no s1018A, no DDO and no s992A hawking prohibition for hospital or extras cover. Because it is not a financial service, the Australian Consumer Law applies instead of the ASIC Act (Competition and Consumer Act s131A carves out financial services only), enforced by the ACCC. ACL s32(1) mirrors ASIC Act s12DE: a person must not "offer any rebate, gift, prize or other free item with the intention of not providing it, or of not providing it as offered".

### 6.2 The broker regime

PHI Act Schedule 1 (Dictionary) defines a **private health insurance broker** as a person "(a) who deals (otherwise than by carrying on health insurance business) in insurance policies that cover hospital treatment or general treatment or both; and (b) who acts on behalf of persons seeking to become insured under those policies." There is no licence for brokers. The Ombudsman Act 1976 gives the Private Health Insurance Ombudsman jurisdiction: complaints may be made against "a private health insurance broker" (s20F(c)), and the Ombudsman may "investigate the practices and procedures of a private health insurer or a private health insurance broker" on its own initiative (s20T(1)), including by inspecting a broker's premises and records. Whether a publisher that links to funds "acts on behalf of persons seeking to become insured" is doubtful on the text, but a comparison service that quotes, collects details and hands leads to funds is closer to it. Compare Club states on its health page that it "is a signatory to the Private Health Insurance Intermediaries Code of Conduct"; that code is industry self-regulation, not legislation, and its text was not reviewed.

### 6.3 The rules on inducements to join

The constraint falls on the insurer, and it is specific.

PHI Act s55-5(1): an insurer must not act in a way "that would result in the insurer improperly discriminating between people who are or wish to be insured under a complying health insurance policy". Improper discrimination is by health, age, gender, location, claims and similar grounds (s55-5(2)); the channel through which someone joins is not among them.

s66-1(1)(c): a complying policy's "only discounts (if any) available under the policy are discounts allowed under subsection 66‑5(2)". s66-5(2) allows a discount only if it is for a listed reason (advance payment, payroll deduction, direct debit, electronic communication, contribution group, age-based, levy, or a reason in the Rules: s66-5(3)) and "the discount is also available for that reason under every policy in the product" (s66-5(2)(b)). A referral code is not a listed reason.

**Complying Product Rules, rule 6**, verbatim:

> (1) For subparagraph 66‑5 (1) (c) (ii) of the Act, the maximum percentage discount allowed is 12% per annum.
> ...
> (4) The net premium is the full premium less the cost, or the cost foregone, of any of the following:
> (a) incentive payment;
> (b) promotional payment;
> (c) rebate; and
> (d) any other inducement whatsoever,
> made available by the insurer to another person, including to an insured person, in respect of the payment of the premium for the policy, including to induce a person to purchase or maintain a policy.
> (5) For the purposes of this rule, disregard:
> (a) a brokerage fee or commission paid in respect of the policy; and
> (b) the cost of any discount, product, service, waiver or other thing (promotion) offered to a person at the time the person first purchases a policy from the insurer if:
> (i) the cost of the promotion does not exceed 12% of the full premium, for a year, for the policy purchased; and
> (ii) the promotion is provided in the first year after the person purchases the policy; and
> (c) any age‑based discount that might apply in relation to the policy (see Part 2A).

Reading these together: a fund may run a joining promotion (free weeks, a gift card, waived waiting periods) worth up to 12% of the first year's full premium without it counting against the 12% discount cap. Anything the insurer makes available beyond that, "any other inducement whatsoever", counts. Brokerage and commission paid by the insurer are disregarded (r6(5)(a)).

The market is visibly built around this. Compare the Market's health page, read 19 September 2026, carries fund-funded offers of 4 to 6 weeks free (7.7% to 11.5% of a year) and one AIA offer of "up to 8 weeks free" split as "4 weeks cashback ... after maintaining paid cover to 4 Jan 2027 & a further 4 weeks after maintaining paid cover to 4 Oct 2027", which keeps the first-year component within 12%. That structure is an inference from the published terms, not a statement by AIA.

### 6.4 Can a PHI referrer offer the consumer an inducement?

**Conditional yes.**

- **Passing on a fund's own joining promotion: yes**, within the fund's rule 6 limits, which are the fund's obligation. The referrer's own exposure is the ACL: the offer must be real and honoured (ACL s32) and not misleading (ACL s18, s29).
- **A code that takes a percentage off the premium for Refer Labs readers only: no, as a fund-granted premium discount.** It is not a s66-5(3) reason and would not be "available for that reason under every policy in the product" (s66-5(2)(b)). A fund could frame it as a first-year promotion within r6(5)(b) instead, which is the fund's decision and legal risk.
- **A referrer-funded reward (for example a gift card paid out of the referrer's own commission): permitted on the face of the text**, because rule 6(4) reaches only inducements "made available by the insurer", and commission is disregarded by 6(5)(a). Whether an arrangement where the fund pays a higher commission earmarked for a consumer reward counts as an insurer-provided inducement is not settled by the text (section 10, item 5).

## 7. Business lending

### 7.1 Outside the NCCP, on conditions

National Credit Code s5(1): the Code applies if, when the contract is entered into:

> (a) the debtor is a natural person or a strata corporation; and
> (b) the credit is provided or intended to be provided wholly or predominantly:
> (i) for personal, domestic or household purposes; or
> (ii) to purchase, renovate or improve residential property for investment purposes; or
> (iii) to refinance credit that has been provided wholly or predominantly to purchase, renovate or improve residential property for investment purposes; ...

s5(4): the predominant purpose is "the purpose for which more than half of the credit is intended to be used". The NCCP Act licensing provisions attach to credit "the provision of which the National Credit Code applies to" (s6(1), item 1(b) and item 6), so credit outside s5 is outside s29.

**Business purpose declaration.** Code s13(2): credit is presumed not to be for a Code purpose "if the debtor declares, before entering the contract, that the credit is to be applied wholly or predominantly for a purpose that is not a Code purpose, unless the contrary is established". s13(3): "the declaration is ineffective if, when the declaration was made, the credit provider or a person (the prescribed person) ... (a) knew, or had reason to believe; or (b) would have known, or had reason to believe, if [they] had made reasonable inquiries ... that the credit was in fact to be applied wholly or predominantly for a Code purpose." s13(5): it must be "substantially in the form (if any) required by the regulations". s13(6): inducing a false or misleading declaration is an offence, 2 years imprisonment, strict liability as to falsity (s13(7)).

**The referrer is a prescribed person.** NCCP Regulations reg 67(c): where the declaration was obtained by someone other than the credit provider or its associate, the prescribed person includes "(ii) a person who referred the debtor to the person who obtained the declaration (whether the referral was for the purpose of obtaining the declaration or otherwise)" and "(iii) a person who suggested that the debtor apply for the provision of credit". So what a referrer knew, or would have learned by reasonable inquiry, about the borrower's real purpose can void the declaration and bring the loan back under the Code, at which point the referrer's conduct is back under s29.

Reg 68 prescribes the declaration's words and the warning "By signing this declaration you may lose your protection under the National Credit Code."

### 7.2 Conditions that follow

| Situation | Position |
|---|---|
| Company, trust with corporate trustee, or partnership of companies borrowing for business | Outside the Code: s5(1)(a) requires a natural person or strata corporation |
| Sole trader borrowing for the business | The debtor is a natural person, so the Code applies if the purpose test in s5(1)(b) is met. Outside only if more than half the credit is for business use (s5(4)), ideally evidenced by a reg 68 declaration |
| Loan secured over the owner's home | Security does not by itself bring a loan within s5, but the purpose does. Any part used to buy, renovate or refinance residential investment property counts toward the Code purpose (s5(1)(b)(ii), (iii)) |
| Borrower's details suggest a personal use (a car for family use, debt consolidation of personal cards) | The referrer's knowledge can void a declaration (s13(3), reg 67(c)) |
| Pressing a borrower to tick "business purpose" | Offence under s13(6) |

### 7.3 What still applies to a business-lending referrer

- ASIC Act: a credit facility is a financial product for the ASIC Act (s12BAA(7)(k)), so s12DA (misleading or deceptive conduct), s12DB (false or misleading representations), s12DE (rebates and gifts) and s12DH (referral selling) apply to the referrer's own conduct regardless of the NCCP.
- ASIC Act s12BF (unfair terms in consumer and small business contracts) governs the lender's contract, not the referrer's page.
- Privacy Act 1988 s6D(4): a business is not a small business operator (so the Australian Privacy Principles apply regardless of turnover) if it "(c) discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage; or (d) provides a benefit, service or advantage to collect personal information". A lead form that passes applicant details to lenders for a fee is squarely (c).
- State finance broker or commercial agent legislation was not checked (section 10, item 7).

Moula's partner page (read 19 September 2026) states: "Business lending is not regulated as a financial service in Australia, and therefore, there's no legal or regulatory constraint to referring a client to Moula." That is correct for the NCCP when the borrower is a company, overstated for sole traders and mixed-purpose loans, and wrong as to the ASIC Act.

### 7.4 Why the business-lending vertical ran into trouble

The vertical was a lead form (`/api/lending-lead`) feeding `matchLenders`, followed by an introduction "to the lenders we compare", with copy that said "If a lender is wrong for you, we say so" (removed from `/how-we-make-money` in `ed23f24`). Three features create exposure:

1. **Sole traders were accepted** (`ENTITY_TYPES` includes `sole_trader`). For a natural-person borrower whose purpose is wholly or predominantly personal, or residential investment, the loan is Code-regulated, and matching and suggesting lenders is credit assistance (s8(a), (d)) and intermediary conduct (s9).
2. **The form collected purpose detail and security information.** Under reg 67(c)(ii) and (iii) that is exactly the knowledge that can make a business purpose declaration ineffective (s13(3)).
3. **The upstream exemption would not have rescued it.** Reg 25(5) needs a written agreement with each lender and a business "not principally making contact with persons for the purpose of giving their names or other details to another person" (25(5)(h)). A lead-capture vertical is that business.

For company borrowers with a genuine business purpose, the vertical was lawful without an ACL. The trouble was the unscreened edge, not the core.

### 7.5 Can a business-lending referrer offer an inducement?

**Conditional yes, for company borrowers with a genuine business purpose.** The NCCP does not apply, so the credit assistance analysis does not arise. The ASIC Act conditions apply (s12DE: the offer must be honoured; s12DA/12DB: no misleading claims). For a sole trader or anything touching residential investment property, treat it as consumer credit (section 4.6): no.

## 10. Points a lawyer should confirm

1. **Credit:** whether reg 25(2A) survives if the referrer publishes a licensed broker's own cashback or offer next to the link, and whether any lender-specific offer can ever be "informing" rather than "suggesting".
2. **Insurance advice:** whether Refer Labs' insurance comparison pages meet s911A(2)(eb) (sole or principal purpose of the transmission is not financial product advice), and whether reg 7.6.01B(3) is satisfied by an internal editorial policy dealing with financial conflicts. Whether a page may rely on 7.6.01(1)(e) for the link and s911A(2)(eb) for the commentary at the same time.
3. **s1018A:** whether the current `/knose`, `/petsonme`, `/knose-vs-petsonme` and `/best-pet-insurance-australia` pages identify the issuer and say where the PDS and TMD can be obtained, as s1018A(1)(c), (ca) and (d) require.
4. **DDO:** what "reasonable steps" under s994E(3) require of an unlicensed referrer in practice, and whether the issuers expect complaints information under s994F.
5. **PHI inducements:** whether a reward funded by the referrer from a commission the fund raised to pay for it is an inducement "made available by the insurer" under Complying Product Rules r6(4); whether a channel-exclusive fund promotion is consistent with s66-5(2)(b) and r6(5)(b).
6. **PHI broker status:** whether a comparison page that quotes or collects details makes Refer Labs a "private health insurance broker" (PHI Act Sch 1) and so subject to the Ombudsman's complaint and inspection powers (Ombudsman Act s20F, s20T).
7. **Business lending:** any State or Territory finance broker, commercial agent or credit legislation (not checked); whether screening out sole traders and residential security is sufficient to keep the referrer out of reg 67(c) exposure.
8. **Life insurance:** whether a life insurer may lawfully pay an unlicensed referrer, given the life risk benefit ratio rules (s963B(1)(b) and the regulations and ASIC instrument made under them), and whether a life referral page is realistic without phone follow-up.
9. **Hawking:** confirmation that no callback or live-chat element is introduced without s992A(5) consent.
10. **History:** whether the deleted consumer lending pages, while live, created any past exposure under s29 that should be dealt with.

## 11. What would settle the open points

- **A written ASIC position** (relief application under RG 51, or informal guidance) on items 1 and 2. ASIC is the only body that can say how it reads "consists only of" for a publisher.
- **Written confirmation from each insurer or fund** that its promotion may be published by a referrer, what s1018A and TMD wording it requires, and whether it treats Refer Labs as a distributor for DDO reporting.
- **A published or contracted referral rate from a health fund.** Without it, section 8.3 is the most that can be said about private health insurance.
- **Advice from an Australian financial services lawyer** on items 1 to 10, starting with 2, 3 and 5 because they bear on pages that are live today.
