## 2. Model 1: an allied-health strength and body-composition service for 40+ (women first)

**What it is.** A subscription service, sold under a separate brand and owned by a separate company. AEPs and accredited practising dietitians deliver it by video. An app (built by Jarred) runs the program between sessions. The proposition is muscle, strength and bone health for women in perimenopause and menopause, then for over-50s generally.

**Why it is not the failed pattern.** The product is clinician time plus software, so there is no inventory. The customer can claim part of the cost on extras cover. It can also be sold B2B to a health fund or employer, which does not depend on Meta ads. Eucalyptus' model was "fragmented clinical supply plus one consumer brand". This applies the same model without a prescription, so the TGA's S4 advertising ban never touches it.

### Market size and demand signals (Australia)
- "perimenopause" is the **6th-largest of 60 longevity terms** tested in AU Google Trends: 3.5x over 5 years, +15% YoY (`longevity-demand-2026-10-02.md`, read 2 Oct 2026).
- "creatine for women" is up 86% YoY, "zone 2" up 44%, "grip strength" up 28% and "vo2 max" up 19% (same report). These show demand for strength and muscle in midlife.
- The government committed **A$64.5M over three years from 2025-26** to perimenopause and menopause care ([Minister Gallagher](https://ministers.pmc.gov.au/gallagher/delivering-first-national-menopause-campaign-australian-women), via the demand report, read 2 Oct 2026).
- Jean Hailes (2023): a quarter of women aged 45 to 64 said menopause symptoms made daily activities hard (via the demand report).
- **Supply is fragmented with no national brand.** A search found only boutique AEP operators: Ultimate Movement for Women ("Menopause Mastery"), OMNI MOVE, Chronic Care Australia (12-week menopause program), EP Online, SeniorsFitness (search results, 3 Oct 2026). None is a consumer brand at scale.
- **Not measured:** search volume for "menopause exercise" or "strength training over 50" in AU, which Refer Labs does not rank for. Pull it in Search Console or Keyword Planner first.

### Regulatory barriers
- **TGA:** none for the service, because no therapeutic good is supplied. The app must stay inside the s14C coaching exclusion. It must not interpret blood tests, DEXA results or symptoms in a way that needs a clinician, and must not claim to treat menopause.
- **Ahpra s133:** does not apply to AEPs or dietitians. It **does** apply the moment a GP, physiotherapist or psychologist is employed or advertised. Keep the clinical team to AEPs and dietitians, and refer out to a GP, to keep advertising free of s133 (no testimonial ban, for example). The ACL still applies in full: every claim needs substantiation (s18, s29), and testimonials must be real.
- **Never name a menopause medicine.** If the service ever adds a GP for hormone discussions, it becomes a telehealth prescriber with all the S4 constraints, and it enters the crowded menopause-telehealth field (WellFemme, Menopause GP, Remi, ALLY, Australian Menopause Centre, per `high-ticket-health-programs-2026-09.md`). Don't.
- **Professional standing:** AEPs need ESSA accreditation and a provider number with each health fund. Claimability of **telehealth** sessions varies by fund and must be confirmed fund by fund (UNVERIFIED).
- **Privacy:** health information is sensitive information under APP 3 and needs explicit consent. Use Australian data hosting.
- **Partner conflict:** Moshy and Juniper both include coaching (memory `moshy-juniper-facts`). Position the service on strength, bone and menopause, never on weight loss. Keep it out of Refer Labs' ranked comparisons, the same separation rule as in `longevity-strategy-2026-10-02.md`.

### Rebates (the conversion lever)
- **HCF Top Extras** (product summary current January 2026) pays **A$40 per exercise physiology visit** and **A$55 per dietitian visit** within annual limits. It also has "HCF-approved Health Management Programs" (including "group exercise physiology classes and weight management") at up to A$200 a person, and "HCF-approved Online CBT courses A$35 to A$59" ([HCF-Top-Extras.pdf](https://www.hcf.com.au/pdf/brochures/HCF-Top-Extras.pdf), read 3 Oct 2026). Funds already approve and pay for online programs. Getting onto an approved list is a sales target.
- **Medicare:** item 10953 (exercise physiology under a GP chronic-condition plan) has a fee of **A$74.55**, up to 5 allied-health services a year, and is available by video under item 93000 ([MBS 10953](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=10953&qt=item), [MBS 93000](https://www9.health.gov.au/mbs/fullDisplay.cfm?type=item&q=93000&qt=item), read 3 Oct 2026). It applies only to patients with a chronic condition (e.g. osteoarthritis, osteopenia), not to wellness. It is useful for the over-50s segment, but it is not the core revenue.
- Insurers are moving into care delivery. Medibank's Amplar holds 90% of 105 MyHealth GP clinics plus 61 Better Medical clinics, and Bupa owns 33 medical centres and plans 65 ([ABC, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-03-23/calls-for-closer-look-as-private-insurers-move-into-gp-clinics/106337010), read 3 Oct 2026). They are active buyers of programs that keep members healthy.

### Capital, time to revenue, margin (ASSUMPTIONS unless sourced)
- **Capital:** A$100k to A$250k to reach about 200 members. This covers:
  - company setup
  - professional indemnity insurance
  - 1 to 2 contract AEPs
  - a part-time dietitian
  - a clinical governance lead
  - brand
  - ad testing

  Jarred builds the app. ASSUMPTION.
- **Time to first revenue:** 8 to 12 weeks. ASSUMPTION.
- **Price:** A$149 a month, the same as Vively's coaching (sourced). Includes an initial assessment, one 30-minute AEP video review a month, an app program with asynchronous AEP check-ins, and a quarterly dietitian session.
- **Clinician cost:** AEP pay is A$65k to A$97k a year, median A$73k (Glassdoor, aggregator, 3 Oct 2026). With about 25% on-costs (ASSUMPTION), that is roughly A$45 to A$60 an hour. At about 45 minutes of AEP time and 10 minutes of dietitian time per member per month (ASSUMPTION), delivery costs about A$45 to A$60 a member. **Gross margin is about 60 to 70%** before payments and software. That is below Hinge's 80%, which group sessions and app-led delivery are meant to close.
- **Automation:** high. The app handles programming, progression, reminders and asynchronous review queues. Clinician time is the only cost that scales with members.

### How Refer Labs' assets give an edge
- **Audience:** the Moshy, Juniper and women's-health pages already reach women aged 35 to 55. The new brand cannot be ranked on those pages, but it can be named in the newsletter and on a clearly separated "our own service" page.
- **Quizzes:** the MatchQuiz engine becomes the intake and eligibility funnel.
- **Partners:** the obvious B2B2C offer is a muscle-preservation and strength add-on that Moshy, Mosh, Juniper or Hims could sell to their members, white-labelled. Whether they want it is **UNVERIFIED**. Juniper's own coaching may make it a hard sell, but Mosh and Hims are worth asking.
- **Software:** Jarred can build the app, the clinician dashboard and the intake flow himself. That is the single biggest cost advantage over a boutique AEP practice.
- **Compliance engine:** the TGA and ACL guards already built for Refer Labs carry straight over to the brand's ads.

### Path to A$1M a year
- **A$1M revenue ≈ 560 paying members at A$149 a month.**
- With 8% monthly churn (ASSUMPTION; measure it), steady state needs about 45 new members a month.
- Customer acquisition cost is **UNVERIFIED**. At A$250 a member (ASSUMPTION), ad spend is about A$11k a month.
- The B2B route reaches the same number faster: **one** health fund or employer pilot of 300 to 500 seats (a fund paying per member per month) covers most of it.
- Profit at A$1M revenue is roughly A$200k to A$350k after clinicians, ads and overheads (ASSUMPTION).
- Getting to "millions" needs either several B2B contracts or a national consumer brand. That is the Hinge path, and it takes years.

### First 3 steps
1. **Demand test before any build (2 weeks, under A$3k).** Put up a waitlist landing page for the separate brand, run small Meta and Google tests to women aged 40 to 60 on the strength/menopause proposition, and measure cost per waitlist sign-up. Pull AU search volume for the core terms.
2. **Rebate and supply check (in parallel).** Call HCF, Bupa, Medibank and nib to confirm whether **telehealth** AEP and dietitian sessions are claimable, and what it takes to become an approved Health Management Program. Interview 5 AEPs, starting with the boutique menopause operators, about contracting or acquisition.
3. **Legal sign-off on scope.** A health lawyer to confirm three things: the AEP/dietitian-only structure keeps the brand outside s133; the app sits inside s14C; and the separation from Refer Labs' comparisons satisfies the ACCC comparator guidance. Only then build.

## 3. Model 2: compliant patient acquisition for longevity and allied-health clinics

**What it is.** A done-for-you content, SEO and landing-page service for clinics that sell longevity services. Prospects include:
- VO2 max and DEXA testing clinics
- AEP and physiotherapy practices
- menopause clinics
- whole-body MRI providers
- the incoming **Ultimate Longevity Center**, which plans 50 AU centres with Double Bay first by Q1 2027 (PR Newswire release of 23 Sep 2026, as cited in `longevity-partner-landscape-2026-10-02.md`; not re-read here), and so will need demand

It starts as a retainer and is productised later into software. The pitch is Ahpra- and TGA-safe marketing that ranks, run on the same guard scripts Refer Labs uses.

### Market and demand
- Ahpra is "now using AI to help monitor compliance" in clinic advertising, according to agency commentary (search result, 3 Oct 2026; Ahpra's own statement not read).
- Ahpra's 2 Sep 2025 cosmetic guideline and the 2023 Medical Board cosmetic surgery guideline tightened advertising rules (`high-ticket-health-programs-2026-09.md`).
- **Price anchor:** one healthcare marketing agency advertises "A$3,500/mo retainers" for Ahpra and TGA-compliant work (Ichelon Consulting page title in search results, 3 Oct 2026, not opened).

### Comparables and competition
- Compliance-checker software is already crowded:
  - AHCRA (free website check; platform pricing "coming soon", [own pricing page](https://www.ahcra.com.au/pricing), read 3 Oct 2026)
  - Clinic Compliance (AI website audit; its site returned 403)
  - Medicom Audit
  - Licet
  - free checkers from marketing agencies (search, 3 Oct 2026)

  **Software alone is not the opening. Delivery is.**
- Practice-management software is consolidated and cheap. Cliniko has 7,211 customer companies and is bootstrapped. A Latka figure of US$1.5M ARR looks implausibly low, so it is **UNVERIFIED** and not relied on.

### Regulatory barriers
- Low for the agency itself. It inherits liability exposure as a "relevant third party" if it publishes non-compliant ads (TGA Code). That exposure is the reason clinics would pay.
- **It must never touch Refer Labs' rankings.** Paid clinic clients cannot appear in Refer Labs comparisons without breaking "rankings are never sold".
- `/partner-with-refer-labs` says "no paid placement". Run the agency as a separate entity or brand.

### Capital, time, margin
- **Capital:** near zero.
- **Time to first revenue:** about 1 month of outbound sales (ASSUMPTION).
- **Gross margin:** 60 to 75% with AI-assisted production (ASSUMPTION).

### Edge
The existing TGA, Ahpra and ACL guard scripts and the knowledge behind them, proven SEO page templates, and the citation playbook. The CLAUDE.md lessons on answer-first pages and owned facts are a sellable method.

### Path to A$1M
**24 clients at A$3,500 a month = A$1.0M a year.** That is a lot of client work for one founder: hiring two delivery staff is part of the plan, not optional. Being a services business, it caps out and sells for a low multiple.

### First 3 steps
1. Write a one-page offer: a compliance audit plus 3 answer-first service pages a month plus ranking reporting, at a fixed price.
2. Send 30 outbound emails to:
   - VO2/DEXA clinics (SSEP, METS, Max Measurements, Progressive; prices in `longevity-demand-2026-10-02.md`)
   - HealthScreen Australia
   - the boutique menopause AEPs
   - ULC Australia & NZ
3. Deliver for 3 paying clients, then decide whether to productise.

**Caution from history.** The `/for-business` service and the Blueprint already tried to sell growth work, and `non-affiliate-revenue-2026-10.md` found **zero** merchant-intent clicks in 90 days. This model runs on outbound sales, not inbound from the site.

## 4. Model 3: Support at Home provider comparison and navigation

### Market
- Government funding for aged care at home is **A$8.8B a year**, with almost half going to the 25 largest providers ([ABC, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-12-04/home-care-package-kickbacks-row-between-providers-suppliers/106095962), read 3 Oct 2026).
- Almost **400,000** older Australians use Support at Home. From 1 Oct 2026, personal care is fully funded under a A$1B measure ([Minister Rae, 1 Oct 2026](https://health.gov.au/ministers/the-hon-sam-rae-mp/media/personal-care-now-fully-funded-under-support-at-home?language=en), read 3 Oct 2026).
- Eight classification levels, reported at A$11,795 to A$78,106 a year (secondary source in search, **UNVERIFIED** against health.gov.au).

### Comparable
Mable, a care marketplace for NDIS and aged care, has reported 2026 revenue of A$19.2M (aggregator, **UNVERIFIED**).

### Regulatory barriers
This is the problem. The ABC reported providers pressing equipment suppliers for "kickbacks, incentives and commissions" after the reform cut provider management fees (same ABC article). Two consequences:
- A comparison site paid by providers would be launching into a sector already under scrutiny for exactly that payment shape.
- Providers have less margin to pay with.

No explicit Aged Care Act 2024 or Rules provision on referral fees was found in a search (3 Oct 2026). Whether one exists is **UNVERIFIED**, and a lawyer must answer it.

Advising on residential aged care fees (RAD/DAP) can be financial product advice needing an AFSL. Stay with home care.

### Edge
Moderate. The comparison and price-table skills transfer directly. Providers publish prices on My Aged Care, so a dated price comparison would be the owned fact. The audience is the adult child aged 45 to 65, which overlaps the Model 1 cohort. Refer Labs has no aged-care pages or search presence today.

### Capital and time
Low capital for a content site plus a navigation service. Time to revenue depends on the payment-model answer.

### Path to A$1M
Not modelled. There is no sourced per-referral rate, and the lawful payer is unknown.

### First 3 steps
1. Lawyer question: can a Support at Home provider lawfully pay a referrer, and on what terms?
2. Pull search volume for "support at home providers", "home care package providers" and "switch home care provider".
3. Ask three mid-size providers whether they pay for enquiries, and how much.

Build nothing until all three are answered.
