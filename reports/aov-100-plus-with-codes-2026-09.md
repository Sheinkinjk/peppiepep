# Programs paying A$100+ per sale that also give readers a code

Researched 27 September 2026 across home, sleep, energy, mobility, fitness, recovery, beauty devices, travel, services and software. Rates, cookies and code policies were read from each program's own page or its own network listing (Commission Factory, Impact, Awin, UpPromote, GoAffPro, Refersion). Prices were read from the seller's Australian site. "Expected per sale" is the published rate times a real order value from that site. Companion reports: `high-ticket-health-programs-2026-09.md` and `expansion-programs-2026-09.md`.

## What the research shows

**Very few programs clear both bars.** Most Australian programs pay 2% to 8% on orders under A$2,000, so reaching A$100 needs roughly 5% of an order above A$2,000. Adding "and gives readers a code" cuts the list again, because many programs cut or cancel commission when a coupon is used (see the traps list at the end).

**The best fit is a program where the code itself is the tracking.** Moshy and i-screen already pay on code redemption with no click, which suits a site whose codes get read in search snippets and AI answers. Proactive Recovery, PlusLife, Ritual, Bon Charge, Kaz Conveyancing and Carbar/Carly all issue codes that can carry attribution. Those are the ones worth pursuing first.

## Pays well but blocked by an existing site rule

- **B2B software** (Semrush A$300 to A$455; Shopify up to about A$225; HubSpot 30% recurring; monday.com): the category-focus decision is no more B2B SaaS. Semrush's "code" is also a longer trial, not a discount.
- **Energy retail** (Econnex A$50 to A$200 per switch; AGL A$50 to A$90): behind the energy-retail hard gate in the consultant brief.
- **Conveyancing** (Kaz, Lawlab): a new category. A third-party summary says referral fees to conveyancers are prohibited in WA, SA and Tasmania and must be disclosed elsewhere. That is unverified; read each state's rules on the regulator's own page before publishing.

## Traps: a coupon can cut or void the commission

These look good but punish the exact mechanism the site runs on.

| Program | What the terms say |
|---|---|
| DJI | "Orders paid with coupons will not yield commissions" |
| My Muscle Chef | 0% on any order using a referral or corporate code |
| Bedzy | 5% drops to 1% on coupon sales |
| Red Equipment | 10% drops to 5% when a voucher is used |
| Renovator Store | 8%, but 5% for coupon affiliates |
| CircleDNA | Codes starting "WA" earn nothing |
| Viator | Discount codes banned |

When approved, ask every Tier A and B program the same question in writing: "Does an order placed with our code, without a click on our link, earn commission at the full rate?"

## Where each fits the site

- **Recovery and longevity pages already exist:** PlusLife, Proactive Recovery, Ritual, Primal Ice, Bon Charge.
- **Sleep hub:** OneBed, Mattress Crafters, Original Mattress, CPAP Organisation.
- **Solar & Energy:** BLUETTI (beside EcoFlow), VoltX.
- **Mental health page in men's health:** Online-Therapy.com.
- **New categories that would each need their own hub and a brand pair with search demand before building** (see the citation rule in CLAUDE.md): furniture, appliances, travel, conveyancing, e-bikes.
