## The two findings that shape everything

**1. The high-ticket clinic categories you asked about (veneers, laser clinics, hair transplants) have no affiliate program in Australia, and the law is the reason.** Every Australian dentist, cosmetic clinic and hair-transplant clinic is an Ahpra-registered business. Ahpra's non-surgical cosmetic guideline (in force 2 September 2025) says a practitioner "must not provide or offer to provide, either directly or through a third party, financial inducements (such as a commission) to any person or company to recruit people to undertake cosmetic procedures", and it names dental veneers, cosmetic injectables and hair transplants as higher-risk cosmetic procedures. The Medical Board's cosmetic surgery guideline (1 July 2023) bans discounts, packages and "a gift or prize for promoting a particular medical practitioner or practice". So a per-patient commission from an Australian veneer, injectables or hair-transplant clinic is unlawful on the clinic's side, not just awkward on ours. What remains lawful for regulated services that are not cosmetic (laser eye, hearing, fertility, menopause, psychology): a referral fee is allowed, a discount is allowed only if its full terms are printed beside it (s133(1)(b)), and testimonials are banned outright (s133(1)(c)). Laser hair removal by non-registered therapists is outside Ahpra, but the clinics that do it also do injectables, which pulls every page about them back under s133.

**2. The money that is actually available this week is in devices and equipment, not clinics.** Australian sellers of ice baths, saunas, red-light panels, IPL and LED devices, rings and beds have published programs, real AUD prices, and orders of A$600 to A$20,000. Four of them can be joined today with published terms, and the best pays ten percent on a A$5,650 to A$20,648 basket.

## Do not pursue, with the legal reason

| Category | Why |
|---|---|
| **Medical cannabis clinics** (Alternaleaf, Polln, Releaf, Dispensed, CA Clinics, Astrid, Candor) | Medicinal cannabis is Schedule 4/8. The TGA's guidance says a third-party link that "draws the viewers' mind to medicinal cannabis" makes our page an advertisement for the medicine, and its June 2026 guidance names "relevant third parties" and influencers. Enforcement: Dispensed fined $118,800 on 22 Sep 2025 for ads "on third-party websites"; Releaf $131,460; Better Leaf $171,972 then Federal Court (VID828/2026); Montu/Alternaleaf in Federal Court since April 2024; Mamamia and Body+Soul sued over paid articles. No clinic has a commission program anyway. |
| **Hair transplants at Australian clinics** (Gro, Crown) | Ahpra 2 Sep 2025 guideline: no commission "directly or through a third party" for recruiting patients. Turkish clinics: Dr Serkan Aygin pays affiliates for USA recruits only; Luviacure publishes no rate; Vera Clinic unreadable. |
| **Cosmetic surgery** (Cosmos, Academy Face & Body, CosMediTour) and **veneer discount codes** | Medical Board 2023 guideline 3.1 bans discounts, packages, time-limited offers and gifts for promotion; 5.2 makes the surgeon liable for our content. No Australian clinic has a program. CosMediTour's travel packages are the exact conduct 3.1(b) describes. |
| **Medical-tourism affiliates** (My 1Health pays $50 to $900 a booking; Medical Departures) | The only payable programs in surgery, and they monetise packaging overseas surgery for Australians. Wrong fit for a YMYL site. |
