# Own-brand longevity products in Australia: regulatory path and launch economics

Read-only research, 2 October 2026. Every regulatory statement below was read on 2 October 2026 from a primary source (tga.gov.au, legislation.gov.au, foodstandards.gov.au, agriculture.gov.au, eess.gov.au, productsafety.gov.au, accc.gov.au, sec.gov, or the company's own site). Prices were read from the seller's own Australian site on the same day. "UNVERIFIED" means a primary source could not be found or read; nothing has been filled in by estimate. Figures labelled "arithmetic" or "assumption" are mine, built only from the sourced inputs shown beside them.

Note on section numbering: the Food Standards Code prints its clause numbers with a long dash; they are written here with a hyphen (2.9.4-7).

## The findings that decide the question

1. **The US longevity playbook is mostly illegal here, by category.** A listed medicine may only carry an indication from the Permissible Indications Determination. That instrument has no "anti-ageing" indication at all, and its only longevity wording is "Longevity tonic", which needs *traditional* evidence ([F2025L00450](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00450/asmade/text)). The TGA's NMN guidance of 12 August 2026 gives "preserving longevity and anti-aging purposes" and "the holy grail of longevity" as examples of non-compliant advertising, even with a compliant label ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/making-nad-nad-nadh-or-nmn-representations-listed-medicines)). On the food side, Standard 1.2.7-8 bans any claim that refers to "the prevention, diagnosis, cure or alleviation of a disease, disorder or condition" ([F2015L00394](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00394/latest/text)). An Australian "longevity brand" has to sell the product on sleep, muscle, hydration or convenience, not on lifespan.

2. **The two flagship longevity molecules are locked to one sponsor each until late 2027.** Urolithin A was added as a permitted ingredient on 19 September 2025 ([TGA regulatory decision notice](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/regulatory-decision-notices/update-listed-medicine-ingredients-september-2025)), but the Ingredients Determination says: "Until 19 September 2027, urolithin A must only be used in a medicine where: (a) Timeline Nutrition Australia Pty Ltd (Client ID 84148) is the sponsor ... or (b) ... the secondary sponsor has been authorised by the primary sponsor" ([F2026L00707](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00707/latest/text)). NMN has the same structure for SyncoZymes (Shanghai) until 10 December 2027 (same instrument, item 3489). The ARTG already holds NMN listings from Caruso's (521492), Nature's Own (530091), Bulk Nutrients (531071) and FutureLab (531277) ([ARTG search](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/artg?keywords=nicotinamide%20mononucleotide)), which suggests SyncoZymes authorises secondary sponsors; that inference is not confirmed by a SyncoZymes source. Timeline's own country selector does not list Australia, and no domestic Timeline ARTG listing was found by keyword search (UNVERIFIED either way).

3. **Hardware is regulated by its claims and by electrical safety, and the recall record is all electrical.** Red light panels sold with pain or recovery claims sit on the ARTG as Class IIa devices made by Shenzhen OEMs (BLUblox ARTG 460946, Apexmed 517949, Ergo Health 526493). Saunas, sauna blankets and plunge chillers avoid the TGA if they make no therapeutic claim, but they are EESS in-scope electrical equipment, and the Found Space, Inner Light and Luxo sauna recalls were all fire or shock hazards ([productsafety.gov.au](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/search-consumer-product-recalls/far-infrared-saunas-updated)).

4. **At A$1M net, the numbers are large.** The two listed DTC health companies with recent filings net 5.5% (Hims & Hers FY2025: US$128.4m on US$2,347.6m, [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375126000022/hims-20251231.htm)) and 6.9% (Thorne FY2022: US$15.7m on US$228.7m, [results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/thorne-healthtech-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2022-results-301786477.html)) of revenue. At those margins A$1M net needs **A$14.5M to A$18.2M of revenue** (arithmetic). Hims spent 39% of revenue on marketing, 34% on paid acquisition alone. Refer Labs' own organic traffic cannot supply that volume: its largest cluster, telehealth, produced 150 clicks in the 28 days to 17 September 2026 (`reports/non-affiliate-revenue-2026-10.md`). Any own-brand plan is a paid-acquisition plan.

5. **Owning a brand changes what the site is.** Every comparison page in the same category would be advertising the owner's own goods under the TGA's definition (a person "causes the advertising" where they "have control over the content", TGA, *Determining if your content is advertising*, 7 April 2026, cited in `reports/tga-audit-weight-women-beauty-sleep-longevity-2026-10-01.md`). The site's hub-neutrality rule and its "rankings are never sold" promise would need the ownership disclosed on every page that lists a competitor, or the brand kept out of those hubs entirely. That is a business-model decision for Jarred, not a compliance footnote.

## Cross-cutting rules (apply to every supplement row)

**Sponsor.** The Act's definition of sponsor excludes anyone acting "on behalf of another person who ... is a resident of, or is carrying on business in, Australia"; the Australian brand owner is the sponsor and may "arrange for another person to manufacture the goods" ([TGA, information for new sponsors](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medicines/listed-medicines/overview/listed-medicines-information-new-sponsors), updated 13 Jan 2026). Listing is via TGA Business Services: "When the application passes ELF validation, the medicine is listed in the ARTG and given an AUST L number", which "does not mean that the TGA has confirmed that your medicine meets all regulatory requirements" (same page).

**Sponsor obligations** ([legislative framework](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-legislative-framework-listed-medicines)):
- Evidence for every indication "must be held at the time when the claim or indications was made and be retained at all times while your medicine remains listed".
- Written agreements with every manufacturer (s26A(2)(i)).
- Australian manufacture by a licensed manufacturer; overseas manufacturers need "a TGA GMP clearance or certification ... Pre-clearance of international manufacturers is mandatory for listed medicines".
- Random and targeted compliance reviews ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-listed-medicines-compliance-reviews)).
- Serious Australian adverse reactions reported within 15 calendar days; records kept 5 years after removal ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/pharmacovigilance-responsibilities-medicine-sponsors)).

**Overseas GMP.** MRA and compliance-verification desk assessments may be unavailable for "some complementary and listed medicines, including sunscreens and dietary supplements. These may not be regulated as medicines in other countries", in which case a TGA inspection is needed ([GMP clearance guidance v18.4](https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-12/gmp-clearance-guidance.pdf), Nov 2023; may have been revised). Fees from 1 July 2026: no application fee; A$818 processing per manufacturer, site and sponsor; A$3,130 compliance verification; overseas inspection A$1,748 per inspector-hour plus travel. A US or Chinese supplement factory is therefore a material cost and delay risk; an Australian TGA-licensed contract manufacturer avoids it.

## Detail by product

### 1. Creatine and other sports supplements

**Classification.** Standard 2.9.4 defines a formulated supplementary sports food as "a product that is specifically formulated to assist sports people in achieving specific nutritional or performance goals" ([F2015L00421](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00421/latest/text), compilation 3, in force 9 June 2026). Schedule 29, S29-19 caps creatine at **3 g** in a one-day quantity ([F2015L00463](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00463/latest/text), compilation 19). Required label statements include "Not suitable for children under 15 years of age or pregnant women: Should only be used under medical or dietetic supervision". Bulk Nutrients' creatine label follows this exactly, at a 3 g serve ([bulknutrients.com.au](https://www.bulknutrients.com.au/products/creatine-monohydrate)).

The TGA's declaration of 23 September 2020 made certain sports supplements medicines from 30 November 2020: those represented for sport or exercise performance (listed uses include "gaining muscle; increasing mental focus ... recovering from workout") that either contain a Poisons Standard or WADA-prohibited substance, or, from 30 November 2023, "are supplied in the dosage form of a tablet, capsule or pill". Products that "only contain ingredients appropriate for food and are presented in a traditional food form will not be affected ... e.g. protein powders" ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medicines/non-prescription-medicines/supply-non-prescription-medicine/reforms-regulation-non-prescription-medicines/changes-regulation-sports-supplements-australia)). **A creatine powder stays a food; creatine capsules with a gym claim are a medicine.**

**Claims.** 2.9.4-7: the label "must not include an express or implied representation that relates any property or proposed use of the food to enhanced athletic performance or beneficial physiological effects" unless Division 3 permits it. FSANZ's open P1010 review notes the EU creatine performance claim "was not included due to the existing prohibition in Standard 2.9.4" ([P1010 paper](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-12/P1010%20Consultation%20paper%20-%20nutrition%20and%20health%20claims%20(5).pdf)). Sold as a general food instead, a product can carry a general-level health claim based on a food-health relationship notified to FSANZ and backed by a Schedule 6 systematic review ([FSANZ](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling/nutrition-health-and-related-claims/notifying-fhr)). FSANZ's register shows 19 creatine notifications, including strength and "Contributes to cognitive function" claims; FSANZ states "Publication by FSANZ does not indicate acceptance, approval or validation" ([FSANZ register](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/fhr)). Illegal here: "anti-ageing", "longevity", anything about preventing cognitive decline (1.2.7-8).

**Making it.** Bulk Nutrients blends and packs in its own Australian facility and says it sources "pharmaceutical grade amino acids from China" (its product FAQ). No Australian white-label sports-powder supplier publishing terms was found (UNVERIFIED). Imported finished food must meet the Code under the Imported Food Control Act; surveillance foods are inspected at 5% of consignments ([DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/food/inspection-compliance/inspection-scheme)).

**Economics.** Bulk Nutrients sells 1 kg at A$48 and 250 g at A$24; Optimum Nutrition Creatine+ is A$39.95 for 360 g at Mr Supplement; Chief Creatine Focus+ A$49.95. COGS UNVERIFIED. See the revenue model section for customer counts. **Main risk:** price competition from an incumbent that manufactures itself.

### 2. Magnesium / glycine sleep supplements

**Classification.** Listed medicine. Glycine is permitted as an active with no conditions; "MAGNESIUM GLYCINATE: A, Only for use in oral medicines"; magnesium amino acid chelate permitted for oral use with magnesium "no more than 25%". There is no separate "bisglycinate" entry ([F2026L00707](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00707/latest/text), Compilation 1, 17 Sep 2026).

**Claims.** Permitted indications requiring scientific or traditional evidence include "Decrease/reduce time to fall asleep", "Decrease/reduce/relieve sleeplessness" (label must say "If symptoms persist, talk to your health professional"), "Enhance/improve/promote/increase sleep quality/deep sleep", "Maintain/support healthy sleeping patterns" and "Help establish/restore/reset sleep-wake cycle (circadian rhythm)" ([F2025L00450](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L00450/asmade/text)). Ethical Nutrients Mega Magnesium Night uses "Support healthy sleeping patterns" on its own page ([metagenics.com.au](https://www.metagenics.com.au/products/ethical-nutrients-mega-magnesium-night-50-tablets)), which matches the instrument. Illegal here: "treats insomnia" (a disease claim), "anti-ageing", "reverses biological age", and any melatonin comparison (my inference from the indication list and Code).

**Making it.** Lipa (white-label formulations, prepares the ARTG listing), Probiotec, Nutrition Care. MOQ, unit cost and lead time UNVERIFIED for all three.

**Economics.** Retail on brands' own sites: Ethical Nutrients Mega Magnesium Glycinate 5-in-1 A$32.50 (60) to A$84.95 (240); Mega Magnesium Night A$49.95 (50); Swisse Ultiboost Magnesium Glycinate A$29.50 (60) to A$79.99 (240); Swisse Magnesium + Sleep A$35.99 to A$56.99 ([swisse.com.au](https://swisse.com.au/products/swisse-ultiboost-magnesium-sleep)). Launch fees: A$1,063 per listing and A$1,566 a year per listing. **Main risks:** pricing against Swisse and Ethical Nutrients, which are also on pharmacy shelves; compliance review of evidence; the subscription churn shown below.

### 3. Electrolytes

**Classification.** Standard 2.6.2 defines an electrolyte drink as one "formulated for the rapid replacement of fluid, carbohydrate and electrolytes during or after 60 minutes or more of sustained strenuous physical activity", requiring at least 10 mmol/L sodium and 20 to 100 g/L of listed sugars ([F2015L00465](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00465/latest/text)). A zero-sugar powder cannot use that name. The TGA's food-medicine tool says a product in a category with a food standard "is not a therapeutic good" ([FMIGT](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/resource/guidance/food-medicine-interface-guidance-tool-fmigt-plain-version)). The market leaders chose the medicine route: Hydralyte ARTG 500896, sponsor Care Pharmaceuticals, licence LI ([ARTG](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/artg/500896)); Liquid I.V. ARTG 531042, sponsor Unilever Australia, 8 Sep 2026 ([ARTG](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/artg/531042)).

**Claims.** As a 2.6.2 food: content claims limited to "Contains electrolytes" or a named electrolyte; health claims only on rapid hydration around 60+ minutes of strenuous activity, at 200 to 340 mOsmol/kg. Hydralyte's medicine label claims relief of dehydration "due to: Vomiting, Diarrhoea" ([hydralyte.com.au](https://www.hydralyte.com.au/products/electrolyte-powder/)), which a food cannot say.

**Economics.** Liquid I.V. AU store: A$22.99 for 10, A$45.99 for 24 ([liquid-iv.com.au](https://liquid-iv.com.au/)). Hydralyte and LMNT AU prices UNVERIFIED (LMNT's .com.au is a "Coming Soon" page). **Verdict:** a low-claim food against two large medicine-route incumbents.

### 4. Colostrum

**Classification.** Both. The ARTG returns 70 colostrum medicines; Health N Nature Colostrum Powder (ARTG 310601) lists "bovine colostrum powder", licence LI ([ARTG](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/artg/310601)). Chief Nutrition sells colostrum as "a premium whole food ingredient" and notified a gut-barrier food-health relationship to FSANZ on 29 May 2026. No colostrum entry appears in the permitted novel foods schedule; a formal FSANZ ruling on its novel-food status was not found (UNVERIFIED).

**Claims.** "Boosts immunity": Schedule 4 grants "Necessary for normal immune system function" only to listed nutrients (iron, selenium, zinc), not colostrum ([F2015L00474](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00474/latest/text)). Preventing illness is banned under 1.2.7-8. The US positioning (ARMRA: immunity, gut, hair, "longevity") does not travel.

**Making it.** Imported colostrum is dairy: from 1 September 2026, "Permits will be issued with the import conditions listed in the dairy review", sourcing and manufacturing countries must be on the FMD-free list, and facilities must be approved by the competent authority ([DAFF](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/biosecurity-trade/import/goods/biological/dairy-review-implementation)). Cottee Dairy Products and South Gippsland Dairy are Australian ARTG colostrum sponsors; whether either offers white label is UNVERIFIED.

**Economics.** Chief Gut Health Colostrum Powder A$69.95 for 90 g ([chiefnutrition.com.au](https://chiefnutrition.com.au/products/grass-fed-colostrum-powder-90g)). ARMRA has no AU store and ships in USD with "Customers are responsible for all import duties" ([armra.com](https://armra.com/policies/shipping-policy)), which is the gap an Australian product would fill. COGS UNVERIFIED.

### 5. Urolithin A

See finding 2. Exclusive to Timeline Nutrition Australia Pty Ltd until 19 Sep 2027, oral only, at most 1,000 mg a day, adults only, "four months or less" duration. As a food, FSANZ's Record of Views lists "Urolithin A 2019 - Non-traditional food - Novel food ... Safety assessment of proposed patterns and levels of use required" ([FSANZ, Sep 2026](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/Record%20of%20Views_September%202026.pdf)). A two-year exclusivity is what a successful ingredient applicant receives ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-application-requirements-new-substance-listed-medicines)); a new-ingredient application costs A$1,370 + A$18,456 (IN1/IN2) or A$3,629 + A$30,067 (IN3/IN4), with evaluation timeframes of 40 + 70 to 40 + 180 working days. No permitted indication mentions mitochondria, cellular renewal or ageing; the nearest are "Maintain/support energy production" and "Helps reduce/decrease free radical damage to body cells".

**Verdict:** not available to an own brand now. Two real openings: (a) ask Timeline Nutrition Australia whether it authorises secondary sponsors, as SyncoZymes appears to for NMN; (b) after 19 Sep 2027, any sponsor may use it within the conditions.

### 6. Protein for healthy ageing

**Classification.** General food, or Standard 2.9.3 formulated supplementary food: at least 8 g protein, 550 kJ and 20% RDI of one vitamin or mineral per serving ([F2015L00419](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00419/latest/text)). Sustagen Everyday is a formulated supplementary food; Sustagen Optimum is "a Food for Special Medical Purpose ... Must be used under the supervision of a healthcare professional" ([sustagen.com.au](https://www.sustagen.com.au/)); Abbott Ensure carries "Clinically shown to improve muscle strength in 90 days!" as a Food for Special Medical Purposes ([family.abbott](https://www.family.abbott/au-en/ensure/home.html)).

**Claims.** Pre-approved for protein: "Contributes to the growth of muscle mass", "Contributes to the maintenance of muscle mass", "Contributes to the maintenance of normal bones", where the food has at least 5 g protein a serve ([Schedule 4, F2015L00474](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00474/latest/text)). This is the strongest claim available to any food in this report and needs no notification. "Prevents sarcopenia" or "reduces falls" would be disease claims. A general-purpose food making a health claim must also meet the nutrient profiling scoring criterion (1.2.7-18).

**Making it.** Burra Foods (nutritional powder bases). Imported whey needs the dairy permit above. **Economics.** Chief grass-fed whey A$119.95 for 900 g; Chief collagen A$52.95 for 450 g. Sustagen and Ensure prices not on their sites (UNVERIFIED).

### 7. Red light / LED devices

**Classification.** A medical device is intended to "diagnose, prevent, monitor, treat or alleviate a disease or injury, or modify or monitor anatomy or physiological functions of the body" ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medical-devices/overview/overview-medical-devices-and-ivd-regulation)). ARTG examples: BLUblox panel ARTG 460946, Class IIa, made by Shenzhen Idea Light, 27 Aug 2024 ([ARTG](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/artg/460946)); Apexmed 517949, Class IIa, Shenzhen Sungrow; Light Tree Ventures 517785, "Cosmetic phototherapy system, home-use", Class IIa, Shenzhen Kaiyan; Ergo Health 526493, Class IIa, Kaiyan, 8 May 2026. Bon Charge AU states its devices "are registered with the TGA in the ARTG as Class IIa medical devices" ([au.boncharge.com](https://au.boncharge.com/products/max-red-light-device-au)). The TGA cancelled OzMask's infrared unit in 2020 because it "was incorrectly classified as a class I medical device" ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/cancellations-suspensions/light-therapy-unit-infrared-35147-cancellation-artg-under-subsection-41gn1-act-0)). Whether a mask sold for "glow" alone escapes the ARTG is UNVERIFIED; "boosts collagen" arguably modifies a physiological function. Electrical: a mains LED device is an EESS Level 3 "Beauty care lamp" needing RCM, an Australian or NZ certificate of conformity and registration ([EESS v4.3](https://www.eess.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/EESS-Inscope-Equipment-Definitions-and-Risk-Levels-v4.3-Approved.pdf)).

**Claims.** Only the intended purpose on the ARTG entry. Acne is a disease claim; the Neutrogena LED acne mask was recalled for possible "retinal damage that could be irreversible" ([TGA, 17 Jul 2019](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/product-recalls/neutrogena-visibly-clear-light-therapy-acne-mask-and-activator)). Device advertisements must carry the trade name, accepted intended purpose and "ALWAYS FOLLOW THE DIRECTIONS FOR USE" (Code s20, per the 1 Oct audit).

**Making it.** Kaiyan Medical ("FDA-Cleared Red Light Therapy Manufacturer for OEM & ODM Partners", ISO 13485, MDSAP; quote form MOQ options start at "500 - 1,000 units", which is a form option, not a published MOQ) ([kaiyanmedical.com](https://www.kaiyanmedical.com/)); Kinreen (17 years OEM/ODM, no MOQ). Because these OEMs already supply Australian sponsors with Class IIa entries, their technical files exist; the Australian sponsor still holds the ARTG entry and its obligations.

**Economics.** Bon Charge AU: face mask A$499, panels A$399 / A$899 / A$1,499. CurrentBody AU: masks A$585.99 to A$979.99, panel A$1,359.99. Margin UNVERIFIED. Fees: A$1,244 application + A$1,305 a year per Class IIa entry. **Main risks:** ARTG intended-purpose discipline in every ad; eye-safety recall exposure; competition from Bon Charge and CurrentBody, which Refer Labs could otherwise earn from as an affiliate.

### 8. Sauna blankets and home saunas

**Classification.** Consumer goods unless therapeutic claims are made. BLUblox's former ARTG entry for a "Full-body radiant heating system heater" was cancelled at its request on 15 Apr 2023 ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/sponsor/blublox-pty-ltd)). Electrical: the EESS Level 3 "Over blanket / Duvet / Wrap" entry covers heat "by placing or draping over the person ... fabric enclosure ... >0.6 m²"; many blankets are PU-covered, so a certifier must confirm. No sauna-specific EESS entry exists; whether "Room heater" (Level 3) applies is UNVERIFIED.

**Recalls.** Found Space far-infrared saunas, 5 Jul 2024: faulty heater elements and connectors, risk of "serious injury, death and/or property damage if the sauna catches fire"; supplier in liquidation (update 5 May 2026). Inner Light Sauna, 29 Sep 2025: heaters "may not be earthed correctly", plug and cord "not certified" ([productsafety.gov.au](https://www.productsafety.gov.au/search-consumer-product-recalls/inner-light-sauna-outdoor-infrared-saunas)). Luxo Kivi, 16 Jan 2024: shock risk. The Found Space case shows the failure mode for a small importer: a recall it could not fund.

**Claims.** Bon Charge AU markets its A$999 blanket with "Burn Calories, Improved Sleep, Supports Recovery" and "natural detox processes". Whether those trigger TGA action is UNVERIFIED; an own brand should not copy them, and Refer Labs' existing pages already make no effect claims for this category.

**Economics.** PlusLife saunas A$5,450 to A$10,990 ("Made in Europe, designed in Australia"); Proactive Recovery infrared A$2,349 to A$2,899. Sauna blanket OEMs: none confirmed. Freight-heavy, capital-heavy inventory.

### 9. Cold plunge tubs

**Classification.** Consumer goods with no therapeutic claim; no consumer plunge was found on the ARTG. The chiller's category is unsettled: get a certifier's ruling before importing. No recall of an ice bath or chiller was found.

**Economics.** Primal Ice tub A$899, tub with chiller A$2,650, chiller alone A$5,800; PlusLife portable A$2,990, Nest A$5,650 to A$10,950, chiller A$3,950; Proactive Recovery PolarBarrel with chiller A$1,449. ### 11. Smart rings

TGA exclusion 14B covers "software, or a combination of software and non-invasive hardware ... to promote or facilitate general health or wellness by measuring or monitoring ... movement, sleep, heart rate, heart rhythm, temperature, blood pressure or oxygen saturation", lost if used "in clinical practice" or making claims about "a serious disease"; "Every feature of software with multiple functionalities must meet the exclusion criteria" ([TGA, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-general-health-or-wellness-software-exclusion)). ### 12. At-home test kits

Regulation 1.3 treats sample collection by a lay person as a self-test where results return to the person "without the direct supervision of a health professional"; self-tests where diagnosis without a professional is inappropriate are Class 3 ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/classifying-vitro-diagnostic-medical-devices-ivds-supply-australia)). "Self-tests for other serious diseases and infectious diseases, including cancer and genetic self-tests, will continue to be prohibited" ([TGA](https://www.tga.gov.au/medical-devices-reforms-review-regulation-certain-self-testing-vitro-diagnostic-medical-devices-ivds-australia)). i-screen emails a pathology request form for collection at NATA-accredited centres, with abnormal results routed to health professionals ([i-screen.com.au](https://www.i-screen.com.au/)); Everlab is doctor-led ([everlab.com.au](https://www.everlab.com.au/how-it-works)). Who signs i-screen's request and the state pathology-request law were not read (UNVERIFIED). Own lay test kit: Class 3 IVD application audit A$25,350 plus A$1,244 and A$948 a year. **Avoid as own brand**; Refer Labs already earns from i-screen on its code.

## The two non-own-brand routes

### A. Exclusive Australian distribution of a foreign DTC brand

**Who holds the TGA position.** The sponsor is whoever imports or arranges import and must hold the ARTG entry before supply. Sponsorship "cannot be transferred without the current sponsor's consent"; the new sponsor notifies the TGA within 3 months; overseas GMP clearances must be transferred with both sponsors' authorisation; a former sponsor can commit an offence by selling stock after transfer ([TGA, changing the sponsor](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/changing-sponsor-therapeutic-good)). So a distributor who becomes sponsor holds real leverage, and a brand will insist the agreement compels transfer on termination. Any distribution agreement must say who holds each ARTG entry and how it moves.

**Competition law.** Exclusive dealing breaches s47 only if it substantially lessens competition (since 6 Nov 2017); in a fragmented supplement market an exclusive distributorship is unlikely to need notification; if lodged, A$2,500 ([ACCC guidelines](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/Exclusive%20dealing%20guidelines.pdf)).

**Typical terms** (exclusivity period, minimum purchase commitments, distributor margin, marketing spend): **UNVERIFIED.** No primary source publishes them; only law-firm templates, without figures.

**What the evidence shows about how foreign wellness brands enter.** ABN Lookup (2 Oct 2026): AG1 USA, Inc. holds an Australian ABN, GST-registered from 1 Oct 2025, no Australian address; Eight Sleep Inc. holds an ABN, GST-registered from 1 May 2022, New York address; Hims & Hers set up its own subsidiary (H&H Australia Intermediate Holdings Pty Ltd, registered 29 Jan 2026). No independent Australian distributor was found for any of them; Oura, Thorne, Momentous, LMNT, Timeline and HigherDOSE had no Australian entity found by name. **The successful brands sell direct.** A distributor deal is realistic only for a brand too small to come itself, and the product must then be one an Australian sponsor can lawfully list (urolithin A and NMN are tied up until 2027, so Timeline-type brands are out unless authorised).

### B. Co-branded or white-label partnership with an Australian manufacturer

Lipa offers both a full private-label service and pre-formulated white-label formulations, and prepares ARTG listing submissions and evidence summaries. Because the sponsor must have "written agreements in place with all the manufacturers" (s26A(2)(i)), the brand owner can be sponsor while Lipa manufactures, which keeps the listing, and the customer relationship, with the brand. Lipa's terms keep each party's IP separate, set price by MOQ, and allow personal or bank guarantees. This is the lowest-friction route to a compliant AUST L sleep product. The alternative co-brand shape, where the manufacturer remains sponsor and Refer Labs lends its name, leaves Refer Labs with a revenue share and none of the regulatory obligations, but no published terms for it were found (UNVERIFIED).

## The three routes most realistic for a small team at A$1M+

1. **AUST L magnesium/glycine sleep product, made by an Australian TGA-licensed white-label manufacturer (Lipa first), Refer Labs as sponsor.** The only supplement here with strong permitted claims ("improve sleep quality", "reduce time to fall asleep"), permitted ingredients without exclusivity, a domestic manufacturer that prepares the listing, sourced fees under A$3,000 to list, and a subscription shape. Reaching A$1M *net* needs on the order of 95,000 to 119,000 customers a year at peer margins, so read this as a A$1M *revenue* first step, with a protein-and-creatine ageing range added later on the same manufacturer and customer list.

2. **Red light / LED device as Australian sponsor of an OEM product that is already on the ARTG through other sponsors (Kaiyan, Idea Light).** High order value (A$399 to A$1,499) means 7,500 to 20,000 units instead of 100,000 subscribers; the Class IIa route is documented by at least four live ARTG entries; and it fits pages that already get impressions (the LED mask page with Omnilux-price intent, per the 29 Sep research). The costs that decide it, EESS Level 3 certification, OEM MOQ and unit price, are UNVERIFIED and need quotes before any commitment.

3. **Become the authorised Australian sponsor or distributor for an ingredient-owner's product: approach Timeline Nutrition Australia about urolithin A.** Timeline holds the only Australian right to urolithin A until 19 Sep 2027 and does not sell in Australia on its own site. If it authorises secondary sponsors (as SyncoZymes appears to for NMN), an Australian partner with an audience is the missing piece. This is speculative until Timeline answers, but it is the one longevity product in this report where no domestic ARTG listing was found and the legal exclusivity has a fixed end date. Expect the sponsorship-transfer clause to be the core of any deal.

Routes rejected for a small team: smart rings (patent and software), own-brand test kits (self-test rules, A$25,350 audit, and the existing i-screen income), saunas (capital and recall exposure), electrolytes (incumbents with medicine claims), and mouth tape on its own (order value too low).
