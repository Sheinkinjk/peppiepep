## 1. Method, and how far to trust each number

**Google Trends, Australia (primary demand signal).** I pulled Google Trends' own explore and widgetdata endpoints for 60 terms, geo AU, on 2 Oct 2026. Each term has a 5-year weekly series and a 12-month weekly series; the incomplete final week is dropped. Then I made a second pull comparing each term against "creatine" in groups of five, giving relative volume on one scale. Metrics:
- **AU vol**: mean 12-month interest relative to "creatine" = 100. A volume index, not a search count.
- **5y x**: mean of the last 52 weeks ÷ the first 52 weeks of the 5-year series.
- **YoY x**: last 52 weeks ÷ the prior 52 weeks.
- **12m x**: last 13 weeks ÷ first 13 weeks of the 12-month series. Above 1 means still accelerating. **Seasonal terms distort this**: Christmas-peak devices (LED masks) and winter terms move it.
- **Low-volume flag**: where more than about 50 of the 260 five-year weeks read zero, Trends is rounding a tiny volume, and ratios are unreliable. Such terms are marked † below. Checked by hand: "urolithin a" has 219 zero weeks out of 261, "full body mri" 216, "biological age test" 254.

**Exploding Topics (US/global, 2-year window).** Read from each topic page on 2 Oct 2026 (explodingtopics.com/topic/<slug>), giving volume, growth and status. Topic pages that returned no data are listed as "n/a". This is US-weighted, not Australian.

**Market sizes.** Where a company filing or retail scan data (SPINS, Circana) exists, it is used. Otherwise the analyst-report figure is shown and marked **LOW**. Analyst estimates for the same market differ by 10x to 19x (menopause supplements: US$0.99B vs US$18.66B for 2025), so none of them should drive a decision.

**Research coverage gap.** Three parallel research passes ran out of the session's 200-call web search budget. After that, sources were fetched directly. Rows marked UNVERIFIED were not re-read on a primary page. **Not reached:** grip-strength devices, posture devices, Chemist Warehouse category data (403), Amazon AU bestsellers (503), TikTok Shop lists, AUD prices for Oura, Whoop, Samsung and RingConn rings.

**Hand checks made (the CLAUDE.md rule).**
- Exploding Topics figures for creatine gummies, spermidine, colostrum and mushroom coffee were re-read: they match.
- Oura's S-1 revenue and member figures were re-read on SEC EDGAR: they match.
- Every Trends ratio above 4x was checked against its zero-week count. The 93x for "creatine for women" and the 58.6x for "eight sleep" are real low-base effects (141 and 73 zero weeks), so they are reported as YoY rather than 5-year figures.

### 1. Creatine for women, over-50s and in gummies

- **AU demand.** "creatine" is the anchor and the second-largest supplement term tested (only magnesium and collagen are comparable). +53% YoY, 3.4x over 5 years, still rising within the year (12m x 1.28; peak week 1 to 7 Feb 2026). "creatine monohydrate" +71% YoY. "creatine for women" +86% YoY (low base). "creatine gummies" +62% YoY, +39% within 12 months. Source: Google Trends AU, read 2 Oct 2026.
- **US demand.** SPINS: creatine sales +71.9% in MULO performance and +48.9% in natural-channel energy support, 52 weeks to 30 Nov 2025 ([Nutritional Outlook, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.nutritionaloutlook.com/view/strong-tailwinds-on-creatine-as-a-broader-range-of-consumers-recognize-its-many-benefits), read 2 Oct 2026). Exploding Topics: creatine gummies 165K, +279%, "exploding" ([page](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/creatine-gummies), read and hand-checked 2 Oct 2026).
- **Who buys.** Bloomberg (2 Sep 2025, via [MedicalXpress](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-09-women-older-adults-sales-creatine.html), read 2 Oct 2026):
  - GNC creatine sales +75% since 2020;
  - women are 30% of GNC creatine buyers, up from 18% in 2020;
  - average buyer age rose from 30 to 35;
  - the Amazon creatine category was projected at US$400M+ in 2025.
  - A "US$4.2B by 2030" forecast in the same piece is an analyst figure: LOW.
- **Leading DTC brand.** Create Wellness: sales US$5M (2023), US$20M (2024), US$40M to 80M projected (2025) (Bloomberg, above). US$20M Series B, Mar 2026; ranged in Target, Vitamin Shoppe and Sprouts ([NutraIngredients, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/03/30/create-raises-20m-in-series-b-to-expand-creatine-access-and-innovation/), read 2 Oct 2026). **No AU store or AU shipping found.**
- **Australia.** Plain creatine is cheap and commoditised (Melrose 300g A$37.95; Switch 500g A$62.96; Naternal, women-positioned, A$40; read from each store's products.json, 2 Oct 2026). Gummies: only Swoleys (A$59.85, Lytes) seen. No AU brand found that owns women, over-50s or gummies.
- **Why first.** It is the only category that combines the largest AU volume, accelerating growth, a consumable with monthly repeat, and an unentered US leader. Protein for healthy ageing (protein powder +27% YoY; sarcopenia +58% YoY on a small base) is the same buyer and folds in here.
- **Unverified.** Chemist Warehouse category data (403). The exact FSANZ status of creatine in a gummy positioned for women or cognition: plain creatine is sold as a sports food, but a cognition or ageing-muscle claim may make it a therapeutic good. **Get this checked before any own-product decision.**

## 5. What this means for "five huge products"

Read with the earlier reports, not instead of them.

- **Own-product candidates (consumable, repeat, no AU DTC leader, rising AU demand):** creatine for women and over-50s, including gummies (#1), and a magnesium glycinate sleep stack (#3). Urolithin A is the exclusive-distribution question to put to Timeline (watch list). All three need a regulatory read first (FSANZ sports food vs TGA listed medicine), because the claims that make them sell ("cognition", "sleep", "muscle ageing") are the claims the TGA regulates.
- **Affiliate or partner categories (strong AU players or a hardware moat, but demand the site can capture):** smart rings (#2), blood testing and screening (#4), red light and LED (#5), CGM ahead of Stelo (#6), perimenopause (#7), walking pads and VO2 testing (#8). Programs for #2, #4, #5 and #6 are already identified in `high-ticket-health-programs-2026-09.md` and `expansion-programs-2026-09.md`.
- **What the evidence does not support:** cold plunge, ice baths, colostrum, mushroom coffee, hydrogen water, shilajit, methylene blue and sea moss are flat or falling in Australian search even where they are large in the US. A US trend that has not arrived in AU search is not yet a demand signal here.
