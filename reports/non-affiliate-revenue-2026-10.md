# Non-affiliate revenue on existing traffic

**Written:** 19 September 2026, for Pepform Pty Ltd trading as Refer Labs (ABN 32 660 008 159). Read-only research: this file is the only change. Nothing committed, sent or published.

**This is research, not legal advice.** Regulatory points cite the primary text or regulator guidance read on the date shown. Where a point turns on degree, it is flagged for a lawyer.

**Scope.** Four models that earn on the traffic the site already has, excluding affiliate commissions: (1) featured or sponsored placement fees, (2) lead sale, (3) newsletter and list monetisation, (4) sponsorship aimed at the B2B audience of the affiliate-programs pages. Fixed constraints from the brief and `CLAUDE.md`: rankings are never sold; every commercial relationship is disclosed; no fabricated social proof or star ratings (ACL s29(1)(e)); health pages never name a prescription medicine (TGA); no model that needs ongoing reconciliation with a merchant; the Blueprint and the Pepform referral SaaS stay closed.

---

## 0. The answer in one screen

1. **No non-affiliate model moves revenue at current traffic.** The whole site took **240 Google clicks in the 28 days to 17 September 2026** (about 3,129 a year). The best model below earns in the low hundreds of US dollars a year on sourced rates, and about US$1,200 a year only if a buyer pays a directory's full sticker price for fewer clicks than that directory delivers. Every model is volume-bound, which is the same constraint recorded in `seo-rank-constraint`: rank first, then monetisation.
2. **The best of them is a flat monthly sponsor slot on the affiliate-programs pages**, sold to an affiliate network or affiliate-software vendor, labelled "Sponsored", placed outside every ranked list. It is the only model that is reconciliation-free, lightly regulated (a B2B audience, no financial product, no health claim), and sits on a cluster whose traffic is growing (0, then 20, then 37 clicks on the hub page in successive 30-day windows).
3. **It cannot be sold while the site says what it says today.** `/partner-with-refer-labs` states "No fee to be listed, and no paid placement" and "We do not take payment for placement". Selling any sponsored slot while those sentences stand would make them false (ACL s18, s29). "Rankings are never sold" can survive a labelled sponsor box, as Finder and Canstar reconcile it (Section 1.3); the two broader sentences cannot.
4. **The operator's affiliate-hub figure is a 28-day figure, not a 90-day one.** Over 20 June to 17 September 2026 `/affiliate-programs-australia` drew **6,153 impressions and 57 clicks** (page dimension). The ~2,762 and ~31 figure matches a 28-day window in mid-August to mid-September (Section 2).
5. **The Apollo arrangement is described three different ways on the site**, and the code cannot tell which is true (Section 3.3). That is a disclosure-accuracy problem to fix before any lead model is extended.

### 1.2 What the ACCC requires

**Primary source:** ACCC, *Comparator websites: A guide for comparator website operators and suppliers*, **August 2015** ([PDF](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/CSBS%20-%20Comparator%20web%20sites%20project%20-%20Industry%20Guidance%20-%20final.pdf); [release, 3 August 2015](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/accc-releases-comparator-website-guidance)), read 19 September 2026. A correction to the brief: the operator guide is dated August 2015, not 2014. The earlier ACCC document is the industry *report*, *The comparator website industry in Australia* ([PDF](https://www.accc.gov.au/system/files/926_Comparator%20website%20industry%20in%20Australia%20report_FA.pdf)); its 2014 date was not confirmed from the PDF itself.

What the guide says, verbatim (section 4, pages 3 to 4):

- "operators who allow commercial relationships with suppliers to impact upon the presentation of content or comparison results are likely to be engaging in misleading or deceptive conduct. Further, where an operator makes representations about their impartiality and independence but then produces search results based on preferential relationships with suppliers, it is likely that the representations will be false or misleading in contravention of the ACL."
- Operators should "clearly and prominently differentiate 'sponsored' or 'advertised' products from the organic search results – for example, by using different colours and including a statement that the product is being 'advertised' or 'sponsored'".
- Operators should "not allow suppliers to pay them a fee (or receive some other benefit) in exchange for the operator giving preference to those suppliers' products in the search results. There is a risk that disclosure may not be sufficient to overcome any misleading impression created."
- Operators should "disclose their commercial relationships with suppliers even where the commercial relationship does not affect the comparison results".
- Glossary, "Prominent disclosure": "disclosure outlined in a set of terms or conditions located in a separate section of the website or in fine print at the bottom of the web page is not prominent disclosure."

Self-regulatory overlay: AANA Code of Ethics clause 2.7, "Advertising shall be clearly distinguishable as such", with the practice note that advertising should not be disguised as "independent reviews" ([Ad Standards, distinguishable advertising](https://adstandards.com.au/issues/distinguishable-advertising/), read 19 Sep 2026 via search result text).

### 1.3 Does "featured" conflict with "rankings are never sold"?

**A paid position inside a ranked list does.** The ACCC's own view is that disclosure "may not be sufficient" to cure paid preference in results, so a paid "Featured" row at the top of `/best-weight-loss-telehealth-australia` would breach both the house rule and, on the ACCC's reading, risk ACL s18.

**A labelled advertisement outside the ranked list does not conflict with the ranking promise.** That is how the large publishers reconcile it: Finder keeps "Top Pick" designations independent and lets users re-sort to a non-commercial order; Canstar says its ratings and awards "are never influenced by who pays us" while selling fixed-position sponsored slots "in or near" tables; WhistleOut keeps the order unmanipulated "with the exception of clearly marked advertisements."

**But Refer Labs has published a wider promise than "rankings are never sold".** In the repo today:

- `src/app/partner-with-refer-labs/page.tsx`: "No fee to be listed, and no paid placement"; "There is no listing fee and no placement fee, and we do not sell position in a table"; "We do not take payment for placement, we do not publish sponsored posts dressed as comparisons".
- `src/app/how-we-make-money/page.tsx`: "Rankings are not for sale. A brand can't pay to rank higher or have a criticism removed." Its intro says "We keep the lights on two ways" but the page describes only one (affiliate links). That sentence is already inaccurate.
- `src/app/for-business/page.tsx`: "Enquire about being featured", next to "Placement never buys a ranking". "Featured" there means listed on merit, not paid; a buyer could read it otherwise.

Any paid sponsor slot requires rewriting the partner page first. The line that can stay is "rankings and comparison tables are never sold". "No paid placement" and "we do not take payment for placement" would have to go, and `/how-we-make-money` would have to list the sponsorship. Selling the slot before those edits would make live text false.

### 1.4 Verdict on consumer pages

**Reject featured placement on consumer health, pet and energy pages.**
- *Revenue:* the only cluster with volume is telehealth (150 clicks in the 28 days to 17 Sep 2026; 334 in 90 days). Assume a labelled sponsor box takes 5% of page visitors (an assumption, not a sourced rate): 150 x 5% = **7.5 sponsor clicks a month**. No Australian rate exists to price that (UNVERIFIED). At SaaSHub's implied US$4.30 to US$7.62 a referral (US$99 / 23 and US$99 / 13), it would be worth about **US$32 to US$57 a month**, and the likeliest buyer is a competitor of Moshy, Mosh or Juniper, the three partners that carry the site's revenue.
- *Regulatory:* ACCC guide section 4 (above); ACL s18 and s29 against the partner-page promise; TGA: a sponsor ad from a telehealth prescriber is advertising a service, allowed only if it never names or identifies a medicine, and every sponsor creative would need that check.
- *Reconciliation:* none if flat-fee.
- *Months to first revenue:* 2 to 3 (rewrite promise pages, sell, build a labelled slot).
- *Hub-neutrality rule* (memory `hub-neutrality-rule`): hubs open with the comparison, never one partner's offer. A sponsor box on a hub breaks that rule directly.

## 3. Lead sale

### 3.1 What a form-to-merchant handoff needs

| Requirement | Source (read 19 Sep 2026) | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| APP 7 applies even to a small business that sells leads | Privacy Act 1988 s6D(4)(c): not a small business operator if it "discloses personal information about another individual to anyone else for a benefit, service or advantage" (quoted in `reports/licensed-verticals-assessment-2026-10.md`) | Any paid lead handoff puts the whole business under the APPs, regardless of turnover. |
| Collecting in order to hand to another business for its marketing is "facilitating" direct marketing | OAIC APP Guidelines ch 7, para 7.43: an organisation "facilitates direct marketing where it collects personal information for the purpose of providing that personal information to another organisation ... so that the second organisation can undertake direct marketing of its own products or services" ([OAIC ch 7](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-7-app-7-direct-marketing)) | Consent must name who receives the lead. |

| Source must be disclosed on request | APP 7.6, paras 7.45 to 7.47 | Keep a per-lead record of where it went. |
| Health information needs consent for any direct-marketing use | APP 7.4, para 7.31; para 7.32 ("applies even if the individual and the organisation have a pre-existing relationship") | Telehealth leads carry sensitive information; see 3.4. |
| Spam Act: consent, sender identification, working unsubscribe | ACMA: businesses must stop marketing "within 5 business days" of a request; obligations cannot be outsourced to list suppliers; bought lists do not carry consent ([acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam), read via search result text; the page timed out on direct fetch) | The buyer's own emails and SMS must rest on consent the form actually captured, with Refer Labs identifiable as the collector. |

### 3.2 What the code does today

**Apollo EOI (`src/app/api/apollo-eoi/route.ts`, `src/components/apollo/ApolloEoiForm.tsx`).**
- Collects name, email, phone, postcode, plus optional property type, has-solar, quarterly bill band, timeframe and notes. Zod-validated; honeypot field `company_website_confirm`.
- Consent checkbox, required: "I agree to Refer Labs sharing my details with Apollo Energy Group, and to both contacting me by phone, email or SMS." This is specific to one named recipient, which fits APP 7.3 and para 7.43 for Apollo and for nobody else.
- Stores the lead in Supabase `public.leads` (`type: "apollo_eoi"`) **before** emailing, via `storeLead()` in `src/lib/store-lead.ts`, then emails the operator (`LEAD_NOTIFICATION_EMAIL`, default `jarred@referlabs.com.au`). **There is no automated handoff to Apollo.** The code never sends anything to Apollo; delivery is manual, from the operator's inbox or the table.
- Sends the applicant a confirmation promising contact "within 2 business days" and "your $500 Refer Labs discount", with: "We may be paid a commission if you proceed, at no extra cost to you."
- Fires GA4 `apollo_eoi_submit` and `generate_lead` events (consent-gated).
- `/apollo-energy-group-eoi` 308s to `/apollo-energy-group` (`next.config.ts` line 203).

**Lending lead (`src/app/api/lending-lead/route.ts`, `src/lib/lending-*.ts`, `src/lib/lenders.ts`).**
- `LEAD_CAPTURE_CLOSED = true`: every POST returns 503 "Business loan enquiries are closed." The comment dates this to 22 August 2026, "partly to review the credit-licensing position".
- The intact handler below the guard would: rate-limit 5 per IP per hour; flag duplicate ABN or email within 30 days; store a full business-finance profile (ABN, entity type including `sole_trader`, revenue, bank balance, ATO debt band, credit profile, amount, purpose, security) in `lending_leads` with consent IP, user agent and `CONSENT_TEXT_VERSION`; run `matchLenders()`; email the operator and the applicant. Status vocabulary in `src/lib/lending-status.ts` runs new, contacted, qualified, submitted, approved, declined, settled, dead. That pipeline is a broker's workflow.
- All lending pages 308 to `/for-business`.

**Listing enquiries (`src/app/api/comparison-listing/route.ts`)** store "Partner application" leads from `/partner-with-refer-labs` (`type: "comparison_listing"`). This is inbound merchant interest, useful for Section 5.

### 3.5 Lead-sale estimate (home batteries, the only viable category)

Assumptions, named:

- Form completion rate: **10%** (assumption; the real rate is readable from GA4 `generate_lead` events or a count of `leads` rows where `type = 'apollo_eoi'`, neither of which this pull could read).

- **Reconciliation:** yes at market terms (7-day rejection). Excluded by the operator's rule unless flat-fee.

### 4.3 What a code-focused list is worth

Assumptions, named:
- **List growth:** 3,129 organic clicks a year (28-day run rate annualised) x an email capture rate of **1% to 3%** (assumption; the real rate is `newsletter_subscribers` rows per GA4 session over the same window) = **31 to 94 new subscribers a year**.
- **Sponsorship value:** Paved's 2.5% to 5% of list size per placement.
- **Send frequency:** 52 a year (weekly).

At a 100-subscriber list: 100 x 2.5% to 5% = US$2.50 to US$5.00 a placement; x 52 = **US$130 to US$260 a year**. Even at B&T's A$352 per 1,000, which is a 27,000-reader trade brand's price, 100 subscribers x A$0.352 = **A$35 a send**.

The list's real value is not sponsorship: it is re-sending a partner code to readers who asked for it, which is affiliate revenue and outside this report. Sold sponsorship in the same email also carries a conflict: the likeliest sponsors are the merchants whose codes the list exists to report.

- **Effort:** low to add a labelled sponsor block to an existing send; the list and sends are the effort.
- **Regulatory exposure:** Spam Act (consent covers messages from Refer Labs, and a sponsor block in its own email is fine); AANA 2.7 labelling; TGA on any health sponsor.
- **Reconciliation:** none for a flat fee.
- **Months to first revenue:** 12 or more; no buyer purchases a list under about 1,000 (Paved's smallest worked example is 3,000).

## 7. The single best model, with its arithmetic

**A flat-fee, clearly labelled sponsor slot on the four affiliate-programs pages, sold to an affiliate network or affiliate-software vendor, placed outside every ranked list.**

- 41 Google clicks to the cluster in 28 days, so about 44 visits a month (a floor).
- 5% of visitors click the sponsor box (assumption; measure it): 2.2 clicks a month.
- SaaSHub sells the same thing at US$99 a month for 13 to 23 referrals, US$4.30 to US$7.62 each.
- 2.2 x US$4.30 to US$7.62 = US$9.50 to US$16.80 a month, **US$114 to US$201 a year**; US$1,188 a year if a buyer pays SaaSHub's sticker price.

It ranks first because it is the only model that needs no merchant reconciliation, carries low regulatory exposure, and uses traffic that is already growing. It is still worth less than one Moshy sale a quarter at A$300 a sale. The larger finding is that no non-affiliate model is worth building ahead of ranking work at 240 clicks a month.

**Before any sponsor is sold,** three pre-existing text faults need fixing:
1. `/partner-with-refer-labs`: "No fee to be listed, and no paid placement" and "We do not take payment for placement" become false the day a sponsor pays. Keep "rankings and comparison tables are never sold".
2. `/how-we-make-money`: "We keep the lights on two ways" describes only one. It should list every revenue type, including any sponsorship.
3. The Apollo arrangement: confirm with Apollo whether it pays per lead, per appointment or per sale, then make `/for-business`, the `/apollo-energy-group` disclosure (which calls an on-site form an "affiliate link") and the applicant email say the same thing.
