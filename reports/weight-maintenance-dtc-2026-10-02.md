## The findings that decide the question

1. **The US "GLP-1 companion" category is mostly relabelling, and its label cannot travel.** Conagra put an "On Track / GLP-1 Friendly" badge on 26 existing Healthy Choice meals and said "no changes have been made to the meals themselves" ([PR Newswire, 12 Dec 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/conagra-brands-introduces-badges-on-select-healthy-choice-products-leading-consumers-to-glp-1-friendly-options-302330416.html)). Abbott's Protality shake "is equivalent to the nutrition in Abbott's successful Ensure Max Protein drink" ([Abbott, 31 Jan 2024](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2024-01-31-Abbott-Launches-New-PROTALITY-TM-Brand-to-Support-Adults-on-Their-Weight-Loss-Journey)). Herbalife's "GLP-1 Nutrition Companion" bundles its existing Formula 1 shake, protein drink and fibre ([Herbalife IR](https://ir.herbalife.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/879/herbalife-launches-glp-1-nutrition-companion-product-combos)). The asset in each case is the positioning. In Australia the positioning is the illegal part: the TGA lists "GLP-1 receptor agonists" and "Weight-loss injections" as direct and indirect references to prescription medicines, and images of "an applicator pen for weight-loss injections" as an implied reference ([TGA, *Complying with the restrictions on advertising prescription medicines to the public*, updated 23 Jun 2026](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/complying-restrictions-advertising-prescription-medicines-public)).

2. **The Australian incumbents already use the label, and serve the on-medication phase.** On 2 October 2026 Optislim ran a "/pages/glp-1" page ("Keep following your GLP-1 program exactly as prescribed"), Youfoodz a "GLP-1 Friendly Meal Plan", and Lite n' Easy "Weight Loss Medication Friendly Meals" naming "a GLP-1 or other weight loss medication" (sources in section 4). Whether food marketing that names the class is "advertising" the medicine is untested: no TGA enforcement action against a food brand on this point was found (UNVERIFIED; not exhaustively searched). A new brand copying them would take on that risk without the incumbents' head start.

3. **Moshy already owns the product this brand would be.** Mosh acquired The Healthy Mummy (shakes, app, 28-day challenges) in August 2023, and healthymummy.com now titles itself "The Healthy Mummy by Moshy" ([Mosh /llm page](https://www.getmosh.com.au/llm); [healthymummy.com](https://www.healthymummy.com/the-healthy-mummy-our-journey-with-moshy/)). Moshy's own weight-loss page has a "Keep it off" pillar with coaching, meal plans and community ([getmoshy.com.au/weight-loss](https://www.getmoshy.com.au/weight-loss)). Optislim, which Refer Labs already earns from through Commission Factory, says its "range can support your maintenance phase" after treatment. The maintenance slot is occupied by Refer Labs' own partners.

4. **There is exactly one pre-approved "weight maintenance" claim for a food in Australia, and it belongs to a meal replacement.** Schedule 4 permits "Contributes to weight loss or weight maintenance" (property: energy), in the context of a "Diet reduced in energy and including regular exercise", only where the food meets the "diet" nutrition-content conditions **or** "is a formulated meal replacement and contains no more than 1200 kJ per serving" ([Schedule 4, F2026C00415](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00474/latest/text)). A formulated meal replacement must carry at least 12 g protein and 850 kJ per serve ([Standard 2.9.3-3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00419/latest/text)). An 850 to 1200 kJ meal-replacement shake is therefore the only product format that can say "weight maintenance" without its own systematic review.

5. **A tapering or "coming off" app is a regulated medical device.** The TGA's coaching-software exclusion (14C) names, as an example of software that is *not* excluded: "Software that monitors a user's weight and provides advice to take or cease taking prescription weight control medication depending on trends in the data", and adds "Where products make claims about chronic disease associated with lifestyle, they are not excluded" ([TGA, updated 16 Mar 2026](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/guidance/understanding-behavioural-change-or-coaching-software-exclusion)). Noom's US "Taper-Off" programme is that product; here it would need ARTG inclusion.

6. **A$1M profit is a A$5M to A$18M revenue business, bought with paid media.** Bottom-up (section 5) gives A$4.7M to A$10.8M of revenue under stated assumptions; the listed-peer net margins used in `reports/longevity-own-brand-feasibility-2026-10-02.md` (Hims 5.5%, Thorne 6.9%) give A$14.5M to A$18.2M. Refer Labs cannot seed it: across 4,494 Search Console queries (33,349 impressions, 1 Jul to 30 Sep 2026, read via `scripts/google-data.mjs`), none expressed maintenance, regain, stopping or tapering intent. Search Console withholds rare queries, so this is a floor, not proof of zero demand.

## 2. Evidence of the need (context only, never for marketing)

- **STEP 1 extension** (Wilding et al., *Diabetes Obes Metab* 2022;24:1553): one year after stopping weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg and the trial's lifestyle intervention, "participants regained two-thirds of their prior weight loss" (17.3% loss at week 68; net 5.6% at week 120; 327 participants) ([PubMed 35441470](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35441470/)).
- **SURMOUNT-4** (Aronne et al., *JAMA* 2024;331:38): after a 36-week lead-in (mean loss 20.9%), those switched to placebo regained 14.0% over 52 weeks while those who continued lost a further 5.5% ([PubMed 38078870](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38078870/)).

Both name the medicine, and neither tests a food or a programme after stopping. The brand cannot cite either in advertising (TGA-Rx, linking and referencing section), and cannot build a claim on them (a "prevents regain" claim has no Schedule 4 basis; section 3).

## 3. The Australian legal line

### 3.1 Targeting "people who have finished a weight-loss program"

- The prohibition bites on "a direct or indirect reference" to a Schedule 3, 4 or 8 substance or class, and "An indirect reference, such as ... 'weight loss injection', is likely to be considered a reference to a prescription medicine when viewed in context. This might be the case even if no specific product ... such as ... GLP-1 receptor agonists is named" (TGA-Rx, above). Advertising "includes any statement, pictorial representation or design that is intended, directly or indirectly, to promote the use or supply of therapeutic goods".
- A "business name that directly or indirectly refers to a prescription medicine may be considered advertising" (TGA-Rx).
- Before-and-after photos: "if it is expressly or implicitly suggested that the 'after' photo is due to the administration of a prescription medicine, this is likely to amount to advertising that medicine" (TGA-Rx).
- Social accounts: "Be careful that you are not inadvertently advertising GLP-1 agonists ... this includes third-party comments, or reviews of your social media posts and accounts" ([TGA, updated 11 Mar 2025](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/regulations-all-products/advertising/specialised-advertising-issues-and-topics/advertising-ozempic-and-glp-1-receptor-agonists-prohibited)). A community of people coming off these medicines will name them in comments; moderation is a standing cost.
- Penalties: civil penalties up to A$16.5 million per breach for a corporation (TGA-Rx summary).

**Answer.** Yes, a food brand can target people after "a weight-loss program" if the phrase stays generic: diets, meal-replacement plans, commercial programmes and lifestyle change all qualify, and the brand never supplies the context that turns "program" into a medicine (pens, "jabs", doses, tapering, "food noise", appetite "coming back", pharmacy imagery, a name like "PostGLP"). This is my reading of TGA-Rx, not a ruling; a TGA lawyer should sign off the positioning line before launch. The tension is commercial: the generic line is the one The Healthy Mummy and Optislim already own.

### 3.2 Food claims (FSANZ)

All read from the Federal Register on 2 Oct 2026: [Standard 1.2.7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00394/latest/text), [Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00474/latest/text) (compilation F2026C00415, 30 Apr 2026), [Standard 2.9.3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00419/latest/text), [Standard 2.9.4](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L00421/latest/text).

| Rule | Text | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2.7-8 | A claim must not "refer to the prevention, diagnosis, cure or alleviation of a disease, disorder or condition" or "compare a food with a good that is represented ... for therapeutic use" | No "prevents obesity relapse". No "works like", "nature's", "the natural alternative to" or "companion to" a medicine. US "boosts GLP-1 naturally" copy is out twice |
| 1.2.7-18 | A health claim needs the food to meet the Nutrient Profiling Scoring Criterion (special purpose foods exempt, 1.2.7-18(4)), and either a Schedule 4 relationship or a notified, systematically reviewed one | A 2.9.3 meal replacement escapes the NPSC; a general-food powder must pass it |
| 1.2.7-20 | Claim must state the food/property and effect, plus a dietary context statement ("in the context of a healthy diet involving the consumption of a variety of foods") | Every claim carries its context line |
| Sch 4, energy | "Contributes to weight loss or weight maintenance"; context "Diet reduced in energy and including regular exercise"; food meets the "diet" conditions or is an FMR with no more than 1200 kJ/serve | The only pre-approved maintenance claim |
| Sch 4, protein | "Contributes to the maintenance of muscle mass", "Contributes to the growth of muscle mass", "Necessary for tissue building and repair"; food meets the protein content-claim conditions (at least 5 g/serve; "high" at 10 g) | Muscle-preservation messaging is available in pre-approved words |
| Sch 4, dietary fibre | "Contributes to regular laxation" (source 2 g, good source 4 g, excellent 7 g per serve) | The only pre-approved fibre effect |
| Satiety | No satiety, fullness, appetite or hunger relationship in Schedule 4 (searched) | Must self-substantiate and notify FSANZ. Precedents exist on the [notified list](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/fhr): protein "helps to increase feelings of fullness/ satiety/ reduces hunger" (The Healthy Mummy, 31 Aug 2020; Sol Cleanse; KRepubliq), soluble fibre satiety (The Healthy Mummy, 24 Jul 2020), PHGG "keeps you feeling fuller for longer" (ATP Science, 18 Mar 2026), whey "Supports weight management" (Chief Nutrition, 15 Oct 2025), creatine "Contributes to muscle maintenance" (Zing Wellbeing, 17 Jul 2026; Menomize, 9 Jun 2026). Each notifier holds its own systematic review; a new brand cannot borrow another's notification |
| 2.9.3 FMR | At least 12 g protein, 850 kJ, 25% RDI of each listed vitamin and mineral per serve; "Formulated meal replacement" is a prescribed name; label must say it "must not be used as a total diet replacement" | Formulation brief for concept 1 |
| 2.9.3 FSF | Formulated supplementary food: at least 8 g protein, 550 kJ, 20% RDI of one listed micronutrient | Alternative for a smaller "top-up" serve; no weight claim |
| 2.9.4-7 | A sports-food label must not represent "enhanced athletic performance or beneficial physiological effects" unless Division 3 permits (protein energy supplement: "may assist in the development of muscle bulk") | Sports-food positioning gives fewer claims than general food plus notification. Avoid |
| 1.2.7-15 | A "diet" nutrition claim must not use a descriptor that refers "to slimming or a synonym for slimming" | No "slim", "skinny" beside "diet" |

The TGA treats a food-form product as a therapeutic good when "the claims being made about them, the form they come in or the ingredients they contain" make it one ([TGA, Weight loss products, updated 15 Dec 2022](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/explore-topic/weight-loss-products)). Capsules, HMB tablets or "metabolism" claims push a product into listed-medicine territory (A$1,063 listing plus A$1,566 a year per product, per the longevity report). Keep everything in powder or food form with food claims.

### 3.3 ACCC

- Claims about the future (keeping weight off) need reasonable grounds at the time they are made, and the business carries the onus of showing them ([ACCC, False or misleading claims](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims), read via search summary; page returned 403 to curl).
- Enforcement precedent in this category: Jenny Craig paid A$37,800 (5 Jun 2018) for "10kg for $10" ads that left out the cost of food, a testimonial from an undisclosed employee, and refund terms ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/jenny-craig-pays-penalty-for-%E2%80%9C10kg-for-10%E2%80%9D-ads), read via WebFetch).
- Fabricated testimonials or ratings breach ACL s29(1)(e) (CLAUDE.md rule; applies to this brand as much as to Refer Labs). A "maintenance" claim also has to state what the customer must do (the diet and exercise context) so the overall impression is not "the shake keeps it off".

### 3.4 Ad platforms (Australia)

- **Meta**: ads "promoting or marketing dietary, health, or weight loss or weight gain products and services must be targeted to people at least 18 years or older"; no "statements of inferiority about physical appearance", no "Close up on specific body area by pinching fat", no clickbait "promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers". When 18+, dietary weight-loss product ads may "illustrate people using the product ... and its impact", with the time taken. "General food products, including protein products" are outside the 18+ rule. Prescription weight-loss products fall under the drugs policy (LegitScript or Meta authorisation) ([Meta, Health and Wellness](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/); [Drugs and Pharmaceuticals](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/)). Separately, ads must not "Imply knowledge of Medical information of a user" ([Meta, Personal attributes](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/)), which rules out "Coming off your medication?" even before TGA law.
- **Google**: "Ads targeting Australia can't use prescription drug terms in the ad or its destination" ([Google Ads, Healthcare and medicines](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en)). "Health" is a sensitive interest category, and advertisers promoting in sensitive categories "are unable to use advertiser-curated audiences" ([Google, Personalised advertising](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465?hl=en)). Whether Google classes a meal-replacement shake as "Health" is UNVERIFIED; plan for remarketing to be unavailable. No bidding on medicine names.
- **Before/after**: Meta's health policy now permits showing impact for 18+ dietary products; TGA-Rx makes any after-photo attributable to a medicine unlawful; Meta's "pinching fat" ban still applies. Safest: no before/after at all.

### Rank 1. Formulated meal replacement shake subscription with a bundled strength plan

- **Case**: the only format with a pre-approved "weight maintenance" claim, a physical margin, and a free strength/habit app as the retention layer and differentiator.
- **Regulatory class**: food, Standard 2.9.3 formulated meal replacement, 850 to 1200 kJ, at least 12 g protein, 25% RDI micronutrients; special purpose food, so the NPSC does not apply. Bundled app inside exclusion 14C (coaching on weight, exercise, diet; no medication advice, no chronic-disease claims).
- **Permitted claims**: the Schedule 4 weight-maintenance claim with its energy-reduced-diet-plus-exercise context; protein muscle-maintenance claim; fibre laxation claim if fibre meets 2 g; satiety only after notifying FSANZ with a systematic review.

### Rank 4. AI habit and nutrition coaching subscription

## 6. Fit with Refer Labs

**Competing or complementary?** Complementary in theory (they sell the consultation, the brand sells the year after), competing in practice:
- **Moshy** already owns The Healthy Mummy and markets "Keep it off" inside its programme. A Refer Labs maintenance brand is a rival to its own non-medical arm, and a white-label or co-brand offer duplicates an asset Moshy bought in 2023. Probability of a deal: low.
- **Juniper** includes meal plans and optional coaching. Whether it sells a standalone maintenance product is UNVERIFIED (site blocked). Its parent's scale makes a white-label purchase from a new brand unlikely, and any clinic email promoting a product "for after your treatment" to past patients is the clinic's own advertising problem: TGA-Rx says emails to former patients are "not provided in the course of treatment and ... therefore not exempt".
- **Optislim** pays Refer Labs commission and already sells the maintenance phase. Owning a rival shake while earning from Optislim on `/health-and-beauty` pages is a direct conflict.

**Selling to their churned customers.** Health information is sensitive information; an organisation "may use or disclose sensitive information for the purpose of direct marketing if the individual has consented" (APP 7.4, [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-7-app-7-direct-marketing)). A partner cannot hand over or market to its churned patient list for a third-party product without that consent. The only lawful version is the partner's own consented offer, which puts the partner in control of the economics.

**Conflict rules if Refer Labs owns the brand.**
1. Separate legal entity, domain, and brand name with no medicine reference.
2. Never listed, ranked or linked in any Refer Labs comparison, hub or `/deals` (hub-neutrality rule; "rankings are never sold").
3. Never linked from pages carrying Moshy, Juniper, Mosh or Hims links: placed beside a consultation link, "after your program" supplies exactly the context TGA-Rx describes for third-party links.
4. If mentioned anywhere on Refer Labs, ownership disclosed in the same sentence.
5. Refer Labs traffic is not an acquisition channel for it (zero maintenance-intent queries in Q3 2026 Search Console). This is a paid-media business.

**Bottom line.** Of the four, only Rank 1 can make a lawful maintenance claim, and it collides with two of Refer Labs' existing partners. On the inputs above it reaches A$1M profit only at a CAC that nothing sourced supports for a new brand. If Jarred proceeds, the order is: TGA lawyer sign-off on the positioning line, a food-regulatory consultant on the FMR label and an FSANZ satiety notification, a Maltra or Formulated For You quote (the missing COGS), then a small paid test to measure real CAC before any inventory beyond the minimum batch.
