# US market assessment: a referlabs.com fork

**Prepared 19 September 2026.** Read-only research. Nothing on the site was changed, nothing committed. Every commercial term below was read on 19 September 2026 unless another date is given. Where a figure is not published by the brand or a network, it is marked UNVERIFIED and not estimated.

## Verdict

**At self-serve rates, the US does not clear A$200k net on any traffic level Refer Labs could plausibly reach within two years.** Hims' own public Impact contract pays US$5 by default, US$30 or US$80 per lead in colour-coded payout groups; at US$30 the model needs about 14.5 paid actions a day and roughly 52,000 buyer-intent US sessions a month, which is about 200 times the AU site's current Google clicks.

**At the higher tier it clears only under two conditions that are both unproven today.** Katalys, a telehealth sub-network, publishes a US$175 CPA default for Hims hair loss and ED. At that rate the model needs about 2.5 paid actions a day and roughly 9,000 buyer-intent US sessions a month (about 34 times the AU site's current Google clicks). Condition one: Katalys or Hims actually grants that rate to a publisher with no US traffic, which its own terms make subject to advertiser approval. Condition two: a new US domain reaches that traffic in a YMYL niche, where Ahrefs' 2025 study found only 1.74% of new pages reach the top 10 within a year.

**The one route that resembles the AU business is a negotiated publisher-unique code paid on redemption.** That is what makes Moshy work (REFERRAL120 pays whether or not the reader clicked). None of the eleven US programs offers it on self-serve terms, and three explicitly restrict it (Nutrafol, Ro, Peter MD). Refer Labs already has a commercial relationship with Hims & Hers through Juniper in Australia; whether it can extend to a US code deal is the single question worth asking before any build. Everything else is UNVERIFIED until Hims answers.

## 1. Payouts for the eleven brands

### How rates were read

Three tiers exist, and they are kept separate throughout:

- **(a) Self-serve, direct network.** Impact's public signup flow (`app.impact.com/campaign-promo-signup/<Brand>.brand`) serves each brand's contract preview (payout, restrictions, attribution window, credit policy) to anyone, without a login. This is the rate a new publisher is offered on application. Read directly for Hims, Ro (as "Roman"), Nutrafol, MangoRx, Function Health, InsideTracker, Ollie and The Farmer's Dog.
- **(a2) Self-serve, sub-network public listing.** FlexOffers and Katalys are networks that sit inside the brands' programs and resell them. Their public listings are open-application rates, but they are the sub-network's rate, not the brand's.
- **(b) Managed, VIP or private.** Custom rates negotiated per publisher. Katalys says "VIP Rates are available on a case-by-case basis for quality traffic partners" on every brand page. No VIP figure for any of the eleven brands is published anywhere found.

**Method note that corrects the prior research.** `research/coupon-site-models-2026-09-07.md` recorded that Impact per-advertiser terms "sit behind publisher login". They do not: the signup contract preview is public for any brand whose slug is known. Slugs that were tried and 404ed are listed under Method limits.

### The two reported Hims figures, checked

**"US$20 to US$40 per new subscription, self-serve": REFUTED as a description of the published terms.** Hims' public Impact contract pays per **lead**, not per subscription, at US$5, US$30 or US$80 depending on an undisclosed colour code. Neither cites a primary source. The US$30 group sits inside the reported range, so the figure is not wildly off for some products, but the unit and the spread are wrong.

**"US$175+ CPA managed": PARTLY VERIFIED, and not a managed rate.** Katalys publishes "Hims, Inc. – Commission Rate: $175 CPA" on its hair-loss page and "$175–$250 CPA" on its ED page. These are Katalys' **default** rates on an open-application sub-network, with the disclaimer "Final rates are subject to advertiser approval and are not guaranteed". The same network lists Hims at US$60 for weight loss and US$35 to US$100 for TRT, so US$175 applies to some categories only. A VIP rate above the default is UNVERIFIED. CrakRevenue, the other network the operator named, no longer shows a Hims offer: its `/hims-affiliate-program/` URL now canonicalises to its generic offers page (read 19 Sep 2026).

**What access would settle it.** Applying to Hims on Impact would show the colour-to-product mapping. Applying to Katalys (their FAQ says applications are reviewed "within 24-48 hours") would show whether the US$175 default is actually offered to Refer Labs. Only a conversation with a Katalys or Hims account manager would reveal a VIP rate.

## 2. What moves a publisher from self-serve to managed rates

No program read publishes a numeric threshold. What is published:

- **Katalys:** "VIP rates are available based on traffic volume and quality" ([RX Media Buyers FAQ](https://katalys.com/rx-media-buyers/)). Applications are reviewed on "details about your traffic types, estimated traffic volume, audience information, and promotion methods" ([Hims page FAQ](https://katalys.com/brands/hims-affiliate-program/)). Its "proven checklist to secure VIP payout tiers" is gated behind a download form ([playbook](https://katalys.com/telehealth-monetization-playbook/)); not read. Its page for media buyers says "landing top telehealth campaigns takes more than traffic".
- **Compliance record:** every Katalys program lists "Misleading Claims", "Unauthorized Endorsement", "Trademark Bidding" and "Incentivized Traffic" as prohibited methods. Ro's Impact terms require compliance with Section 5 of the FTC Act and the Endorsement Guides. Peter MD terminates for "Failure to disclose the affiliate relationship for any promotion that qualifies as an endorsement". A clean record is a condition of staying in, before it is a lever for a better rate.
- **Code access:** Ro only credits codes "provided to your publisher account through this platform"; Peter MD only allows a coupon to publishers who are "pre-approved / assigned". A publisher-unique code is a managed-tier privilege in the US, not a self-serve feature.
- **Exclusivity, conversion history, content-quality scores:** no published terms found for any of the eleven brands. UNVERIFIED.

**Does Refer Labs meet the bar? No, and not on any published criterion.**
- **US traffic: none.** Search Console for 21 Aug to 17 Sep 2026 shows 28,812 impressions and 244 clicks, all on referlabs.com.au and Australian-intent queries. These brands serve US patients only; Ro's terms even cut the lead payout to US$2.50 for "Unlicensed States", so geography is priced.
- **Conversion history: none in the US**, and none in AU that a US program can see. AU transactions are not joined to pages (`reports/h0-code-hypothesis-2026-10.md`).
- **Compliance record:** the AU site's disclosure discipline, no-invented-ratings rule and TGA practice are an asset in a conversation, but they are Australian evidence, not US performance history.
- **The one relevant asset is the existing Hims & Hers relationship** through Juniper (Hims has owned Juniper since 2 Jun 2026, per project notes; `reports/attribution-gap-2026-10.md` records that the new Hims agreement attributes by code). Whether that agreement or contact can extend to the US is UNVERIFIED.

## 3. US regulatory constraints on content

Primary sources, read 19 Sep 2026. This is a list for US counsel, not legal advice.

1. **FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255.** § 255.5: a material connection "must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously", and material connections "can include monetary payment". § 255.1(e): "Endorsers may be liable for statements made in the course of their endorsements". § 255.1(d): advertisers must "Monitor their endorsers' compliance", which is why brands terminate publishers for disclosure failures. ([eCFR, Part 255](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255))
2. **FTC Consumer Review and Testimonial Rule, 16 CFR Part 465.** § 465.2 makes it a violation "to write, create, or sell a consumer review, consumer testimonial, or celebrity testimonial" that misrepresents that the reviewer exists or used the product. It carries civil penalties, which the Endorsement Guides alone do not. The AU no-fabrication rule already complies. ([eCFR, Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-465))
3. **FTC enforcement against telehealth advertising.**
   - **NextMed** (complaint July 2025, final order 3 Dec 2025): weight-loss telehealth; "making unsubstantiated claims about the weight loss achieved by their clients, using fake testimonials, and unfairly distorting consumer reviews"; US$150,000. ([FTC final order release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-approves-final-order-against-telehealth-provider-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-deceptive), [complaint release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/07/ftc-takes-action-against-telemedicine-firm-nextmed-over-charges-it-used-misleading-prices-fake))
   - **Cerebral** (April 2024): more than US$7 million, a ban on using health data for most advertising, and cancellation terms. ([FTC release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/proposed-ftc-order-will-prohibit-telehealth-firm-cerebral-using-or-disclosing-sensitive-data))
   - NextMed's pricing count (an advertised monthly price that excluded the medicine, labs and consult) is the direct risk for a comparison publisher: a price table that repeats a brand's headline price without its exclusions republishes the deception.
4. **FDA on compounded drug promotion.** On 3 March 2026 the FDA issued 30 warning letters to telehealth companies over compounded weight-loss drug marketing, citing "claims implying sameness with FDA-approved products" and branding that implies the telehealth firm is the compounder ([FDA press announcement](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-warns-30-telehealth-companies-against-illegal-marketing-compounded-glp-1s)). FDA's guidance page (current as of 15 Jun 2026) lists five misleading claim types, including describing a compounded drug "as a generic version of an FDA-approved drug" or as "FDA-approved" ([FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-telehealth-companies-what-know-when-promoting-compounded-drugs)). The page does not address affiliates; a publisher repeating a merchant's sameness claim is repeating the claim FDA calls misleading. The AU rule (describe the service, never the medicine) is a workable US posture too, though the legal basis differs.
5. **State patient-brokering and fee-splitting law.** Florida § 817.505 makes it unlawful for "any person" to "Solicit or receive a commission ... in return for referring a patient or patronage to or from a health care provider" ([Fla. Stat. 817.505](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0800-0899/0817/Sections/0817.505.html)), with exceptions that include payments to a free consumer "information service" meeting four conditions, one of which is not steering the consumer to a particular provider. A per-patient CPA from a telehealth provider is exactly the payment shape these laws address. Other states have similar statutes (for example California Business and Professions Code § 650, not read in full here). Function Health's FAQ shows brands price this risk: licensed practitioners "aren't eligible for referral fees due to regulations". **Get US counsel's view on per-acquisition telehealth payments before accepting any.**
6. **State consumer health data law.** Washington's My Health My Data Act defines "consumer health data" to include "Use or purchase of prescribed medication" and applies to any entity that "produces or provides products or services that are targeted to consumers in Washington" ([RCW 19.373.010](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010)). A US quiz or match tool that collects health conditions (the AU MatchQuiz pattern) would bring the publisher inside it.
7. **LegitScript and Google Ads.** Google requires advertisers of prescription drug services to be certified ("Advertisers must be certified by Google in order to serve ads for prescription drug services"), sometimes via LegitScript ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15598647?hl=en)). The policy does not say whether an affiliate or lead generator can be certified. None of the eleven programs' terms read here requires a publisher to hold LegitScript certification. The practical effect is narrower: paid search is closed anyway, because Ro prohibits Search, Peter MD bans PPC without written permission, and Katalys lists Paid Search as prohibited for Peter MD. A US build is organic-only, like AU.

### Code-deal scenario (the AU mechanism, if a US brand agreed to it)

For reference only, since no US program offers it on self-serve terms. Over 20 Jun to 17 Sep 2026 the ten AU pages carrying REFERRAL120 or REFERAL55 in their Google title or description received 19,446 impressions (`reports/attribution-gap-2026-10.md`), while the site recorded about 207 transactions (2.3 x 90). That is about 1.06 transactions per 100 code-page impressions, but it counts redemptions from every source (AI answers, aggregators, Moshy's own channels), so it is not a conversion rate and should not be projected. What it shows is directional: with a code paid on redemption, revenue follows code distribution rather than clicks, and the traffic bar in the table above stops being the binding constraint.

### Time to traffic

- Ahrefs (published 15 May 2025, 1.3 million US keywords, URLs observed Sept 2023): "Only 1.74% of newly published pages rank in the top 10 within a year (down from 5.7% in 2017)", and "The average #1 ranking page is 5 years old" ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)).
- The AU site is the internal comparator: a domain whose ABN dates from 16 Aug 2022, pivoted to consumer comparison in July 2026, with most pages at positions 16 to 24 and 244 clicks a month. A new US domain would start below that, with no backlinks and no brand search, against US health publishers in a YMYL niche.
- **Realistic reading:** on that evidence, reaching 9,000 buyer-intent sessions a month on a new US domain is a two-year-plus project at best, and no source supports a shorter figure. No time estimate is given for the self-serve tiers, because their required volume (about 20,000 to 52,000 sessions a month) is outside anything the evidence supports for a single operator.

### Does the US clear A$200k net?

- **Self-serve rates, no code: no.** It needs 60 to 200 times AU's current Google clicks, in a harder market, on a new domain.
- **Katalys US$175 default: only if two unproven things both hold:** the rate is granted to Refer Labs, and a new domain reaches about 9,000 buyer-intent US sessions a month. Neither is likely inside 24 months, and the model's conversion input is itself an assumption.
- **Negotiated publisher-unique code paid on redemption: plausibly, and this is the only route that matches how the AU business actually earns.** It depends entirely on a brand agreeing to it. The Hims & Hers relationship through Juniper is the only existing lever.

**Capacity.** The AU business is already in the A$168k to A$252k gross band on the stated transaction rate, and the attribution gaps in `reports/attribution-gap-2026-10.md` (the Juniper code, the PetsOnMe tracked link) are revenue that needs no new market. For one operator, a US build is a second workstream competing with those.

**The cheapest test before any build:** ask Hims & Hers whether the Juniper relationship can include a US publisher-unique code paid on redemption, and at what rate. Separately, apply to Katalys to learn whether the US$175 default is offered to a publisher with no US traffic. Both are emails, and both answers change the model more than any traffic assumption does.
