**Prepared for:** external regulatory/legal review
**Prepared by:** Refer Labs (Pepform Pty Ltd t/a Refer Labs, ABN 32 660 008 159)
**Site:** referlabs.com.au
**Audit date:** 23 July 2026
**Contact:** jarred@referlabs.com.au

This brief exists so a reviewer can scope the work quickly. It sets out exactly
what we publish, how we earn, what we have already done to stay inside the
Advertising Code, and the specific questions we need answered. Nothing in this
document is a legal conclusion. Where we state our understanding of the law it is
flagged as an assumption for you to confirm or correct.

---

## 1. What we need from you

We publish consumer comparison content about hair-loss and weight-management
telehealth services in Australia, and we earn affiliate commission when a reader
signs up to a provider. Some of that content names prescription-only substances.

**We want a written view on whether our current pages constitute "advertising" of
prescription-only medicines to the public, and if so, what must change.**

We are about to increase marketing spend on this content. We would rather change
it first than defend it later.

---

## 2. Business model (material to the "advertising" test)

- Refer Labs is an independent comparison publisher. We are **not** a pharmacy,
  prescriber, telehealth provider, or sponsor of any therapeutic good.
- We hold no stock, dispense nothing, and give no clinical advice.
- We are paid a **commission by the telehealth provider** (Mosh, Moshy, Dense)
  when a reader signs up. Commission is disclosed on every affected page.
- We do not sell rankings, and no provider can pay for favourable placement.

The commercial relationship is the reason we are seeking review: it is what could
convert "information" into "promotion of supply".

## 4. Mitigations already in place (verified by audit, 23 July 2026)

Every page listed above was checked live. All of the following are present on
**14 of 14** pages tested:

1. **Calls to action promote the service, never the medicine.** Verified CTA
   labels: "Start a Mosh assessment", "Start assessment", "Check your eligibility
   on Moshy", "Take the 30-second match". No page contains a CTA to buy, obtain,
   or be supplied a named substance.
2. **Information-only disclaimer** ("this is general information, not medical
   advice") on every page.
3. **Practitioner-assessment framing** on every page: suitability is decided by a
   registered Australian practitioner.
4. **Affiliate/commission disclosure** on every page.
5. **No efficacy guarantees.** We searched for guarantee-style language across all
   pages; every match was language *denying* guarantees, e.g. "suitability is
   practitioner-decided and never guaranteed" and "treat any promise of a
   guaranteed outcome as a red flag". No outcome, cure, or success-rate claim.
6. **No fabricated social proof.** We publish no testimonials, no customer names,
   no personas, and no star ratings of our own — a deliberate ACL s29(1)(e)
   position. Third-party figures are attributed to their source.
7. **Warnings against non-compliant competitors** appear on the substance pages,
   e.g. that anyone offering guaranteed access before an assessment is not
   operating compliantly.

## 5. Questions we need answered

1. **Does naming a Schedule 4 substance in editorial comparison content, on a page
   monetised by commission from a telehealth provider, constitute advertising a
   prescription-only medicine to the public** under the Therapeutic Goods Act 1989
   and the Therapeutic Goods (Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code) Instrument 2021?

2. If yes, **does the service-framed CTA cure it, or is the substance naming itself
   the problem** regardless of how the CTA is worded?

3. **Does a substance-named URL and page title** (`/finasteride-australia`) sit
   differently from the same substance mentioned inside a broader comparison page?

4. **Is our understanding of minoxidil scheduling correct** (topical vs oral), and
   does that change the treatment of `/minoxidil-australia`?

5. **Does the commission relationship alone** make otherwise-balanced information
   promotional, irrespective of tone?

6. Are there **exemptions we can rely on** for balanced consumer information about
   prescription medicines, and what conditions attach to them?

7. Anything else you consider non-compliant that we have not asked about,
   including the weight-management content's treatment of obesity, which we
   understand may be a restricted representation.

---

## 6. What we will do with your answer

So the engagement can stay narrow, here is our decision tree:

- **Compliant as-is** → we proceed with marketing, and re-review annually or on
  material content change.
- **Compliant with wording changes** → we implement your redlines across all 14
  pages; they share components, so changes propagate cheaply.
- **Substance-named pages are not defensible** → we retire the six highest-risk
  URLs with 301 redirects to the service-level hubs and keep the substance
  references only inside broader comparison content.
- **No substance naming at all is defensible** → we strip molecule names sitewide
  and rebuild the affected pages around symptoms, services, and cost comparison.

We would appreciate the answer in a form we can act on directly: page-level
redlines rather than general principles, where practical.
