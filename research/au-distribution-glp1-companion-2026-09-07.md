## The finding that governs the whole category

**The product names themselves are probably unlawful to advertise in Australia.**

"GLP-1 Companion", "GLP-1 Nutritional Support System", "For GLP-1 & Semaglutide Weight Loss Support" are the on-pack and retail names these brands use in the US. Under the Therapeutic Goods Act, advertising that references a class of prescription medicines, including indirectly or through substitute terms, is prohibited. This project's own standing rule already treats indirect identifiers such as "weight-loss injections" as advertising the medicine. A product named after the medicine class is a more direct reference than that, not a lesser one.

Every brand below would need a renamed Australian SKU and rewritten pack copy before a single unit could be advertised. That is a real cost and a real barrier, and it applies before any of the FSANZ or TGA questions in Part 2. It also cuts the other way: it is a barrier to every competing importer too, which is part of what makes an exclusive territory worth holding.

**I am not a lawyer and this needs advice before money is committed.**

## Part 2: Compliance assessment for the three most promising

### The rule that actually applies, and a correction to the brief

The brief describes "the November 2023 reform that moved capsules and tablets making performance claims to the TGA and explicitly excluded powders and liquids." That is substantially right but narrower than stated, and the narrowing matters.

The TGA declaration covers **sports supplements that make claims relating to performance in sport, exercise or recreational activity** and are in a medicinal dosage form (tablet, capsule, pill). Transition ended **30 November 2023**. Products in traditional food forms, powders, beverages and bars, are excluded ([TGA: Changes to the regulation of sports supplements](https://www.tga.gov.au/products/medicines/non-prescription-medicines/supply-non-prescription-medicine/reforms-regulation-non-prescription-medicines/changes-regulation-sports-supplements-australia), [TGA news article](https://www.tga.gov.au/news/news-articles/sports-supplements-declared-be-medicines)).

**A weight-management companion product does not make a sports-performance claim, so this declaration is probably not the operative trigger.** The operative trigger is the general one: if a product makes a therapeutic claim (preventing nutrient deficiency, preserving muscle during weight loss, supporting a medicine), it is a therapeutic good regardless of format, and format only decides how easily it falls in. Relying on "we are a powder, so we are food" would be the wrong test.

### Unjury

| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Food or therapeutic good? | **Food under FSANZ if sold as protein powder with nutrition-content claims only.** Powder is a traditional food form and outside the sports-supplement declaration. Becomes a therapeutic good the moment it claims to treat, prevent or support a medical condition or a medicine. Unjury's US positioning is bariatric and oncology clinical nutrition, which is claim-heavy and would need stripping back. |
| Ingredients permitted? | **UNKNOWN.** Not assessed. Requires the full ingredient and additive list checked against the Food Standards Code, particularly Schedule 15 (permitted additives) and any novel-food status. |
| Label changes | Nutrition Information Panel in FSANZ format (per serve and per 100 g), allergen declaration under Standard 1.2.3 including Plain English Allergen Labelling, Country of Origin Labelling for an imported food, and claims compliance under Standard 1.2.7. **Confirm current PEAL transition status before printing.** |
| Claims to remove | Any reference to a medicine class or brand. Any bariatric-surgery or oncology therapeutic claim. Any implied treatment outcome. Under ACL s18 and s29, comparative and performance claims need substantiation held before publication. |
| Cost and timeline | **UNKNOWN.** Do not budget from a guess. Components to quote: regulatory review of one SKU, ingredient/additive assessment, artwork redraw and NIP generation, PEAL review, legal sign-off on claims, and import permit/biosecurity assessment for the ingredient profile. |

## Part 3: Comparable deals

**I did not find verified, named Australian exclusive-distribution deals for overseas supplement brands with disclosed commercial terms.** Those agreements are private and are not published. Rather than estimate numbers, here is the structure that Australian practice guidance and one filed agreement actually show.

| Element | What the sources show |
|---|---|
| Exclusivity | Granted as "the sole right to sell specified products within a defined territory for a set period", and normally **tied to performance** rather than granted outright ([Sprintlaw: Distribution Agreements in Australia](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/distribution-agreements-australia-essential-guide-distributors-business-launches/)) |
| Minimum orders | Two layers are common: an **annual minimum purchase obligation** to retain exclusivity, plus a **per-purchase-order MOQ** to keep order sizes efficient for the supplier ([Sprintlaw template guidance](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/distribution-agreement-template-what-to-include-in-australia/)) |
| Failure to hit targets | Standard remedy ladder, quoted from a filed agreement: "convert the appointment to non-exclusive, reduce the Exclusive Territory, or terminate this Agreement on [60] days' notice" ([SEC-filed Australia Distribution Agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1108271/000119312504202992/dex1031.htm)) |
| Marketing commitments | Typically minimum spend or minimum active accounts, expressed as a performance condition of exclusivity ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d29cc166-d0dd-4242-b69a-40e6350cae6a)) |
| Term length | **UNKNOWN** as a market norm. Not verifiable from public sources. |

The practical read: exclusivity in this market is not something a distributor is given, it is something held by hitting a number. Any approach to Unjury should therefore lead with a volume commitment, because that is the currency the agreement will be written in.

## What to do, in order

1. **Approach Unjury through its International Wholesale Inquiry form.** It is the only brand in the set with a proven product, a completely clean Australian register, no Australian presence, and a published international wholesale route. Ask three things before anything else: whether Australia is unallocated, what annual volume converts to exclusivity, and whether they will supply an unbranded or renamed SKU for the Australian market.
2. **Get the naming question answered by a lawyer before spending anything.** If the medicine-class reference cannot be removed from pack and marketing, the category is not importable regardless of which brand you pick. This gates everything.
3. **Tell Supergut US that NUVOFIBR holds their name in Australia.** Costs nothing, and opens a conversation from a position of having done real work.
4. **Park Replenza until it has a trade channel.** Clean register, but no wholesale page, no partnership contact, and a tablet SKU that likely needs ARTG listing.
5. **Drop Thorne, Nature Made, Bodiez and Supergut.** Owner-filed marks, multinational parents, or an existing Australian holder. None is obtainable.
